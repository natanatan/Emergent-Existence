# 6.16 Stability Without Rigidity

A closed organization that cannot vary would be a poor precursor to memory, orientation, return or dynamics. Closure must therefore preserve identity without requiring every constitutive relation to remain fixed.

Let V<sub>F</sub> denote the set of admissible variations that preserve F. A dynamically useful closure requires:

▪ V<sub>F</sub> ≠ ∅

and should include at least one non-identity transformation. At the same time, not every variation can be admitted, or F ceases to distinguish the organization from arbitrary alternatives. The resulting principle is qualitative but important: persistent higher-order identity requires constrained freedom, not maximal constraint.

Montévil and Mossio’s closure-of-constraints framework reaches a related conclusion in a biological context by treating stability and variation as complementary rather than opposed. The present framework borrows only that structural lesson. It does not import biological function, metabolism or thermodynamic timescales into Closure.

The computational question is sharper: across candidate update rules, do organizations with internally completed dependencies occupy more robust regions of relational state space than equally connected but externally dependent controls? That is a testable claim about our model, not yet a claim about nature.
