# 4.14 Identity Criteria Without an Anchor

The rest of the book also requires a way to say that two changing configurations instantiate the same higher-order identity. That scaffold can be introduced without privileging Ω or introducing a central anchor.

Let F be an identity criterion: a declared structural condition specifying which features are constitutive of the higher-order identity under consideration. Define identity equivalence by

Σ ~<sub>F</sub> Σ′ iff Σ and Σ′ satisfy the same criterion F

The corresponding candidate identity is the equivalence class

O = \[Σ\]<sub>~F</sub>

This is a minimal identity scaffold, not yet a fully integrated object. At the primitive pair the construction is thin; Relation will give it substantive content by supplying several retained relations whose organization can jointly satisfy F. What Boundary earns is the grammar of identity through change: token variation can occur while an identity criterion remains satisfied.

No anchor is required by this definition. A later closed organization may earn an endogenous reference role, but reference is not constitutive of ~<sub>F</sub> itself.
