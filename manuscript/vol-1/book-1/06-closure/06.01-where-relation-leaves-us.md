# 6.1 Where Relation Leaves Us

Relation gave us patterns that can persist. Closure asks us to change scale of view. Instead of inspecting each dependency separately, we ask when the network of dependencies becomes sufficient to hold a higher-order identity together.

The visual temptation is immediate: a loop, shell, or enclosed region. We cannot use that picture yet. Nothing spatial has been earned.

So we look for a non-geometric threshold. When do the relations required to preserve an organization come from within that organization itself?

The chapter’s foundational question is therefore not “what shape closes?” but:

When do the dependencies required for a persistent relational organization become sufficiently internal to that organization that the organization can function as a higher-order relatum?

This phrasing changes the burden. We do not begin from a triangle, simplex, tetrahedron, K4, or a target dimensionality. Those may become useful later mathematical realizations. They cannot define primitive closure without inserting geometric structure the book is trying to earn.
