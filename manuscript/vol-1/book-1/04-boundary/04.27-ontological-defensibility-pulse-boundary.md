# 4.27 Ontological Defensibility Pulse: Boundary

| **Pulse question** | **Applied to Boundary** |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Entities required | No new modeled substance is introduced. The chapter adds structural predicates and relations over already earned configurations: W, Pres<sub>W</sub>, 𝒫, ≺, F and the equivalence relations they induce. 𝔗<sub>test</sub> remains diagnostic unless later physics reifies it. |
| 2. Ontological level | The chapter operates between internal succession and multi-relation organization. Its subject is robustness, dependency, and identity criteria, not geometry or physical dynamics in spacetime. |
| 3. Lower-order dependencies | Every result depends on primitive Difference plus admissible succession from Chapters 2–3. Without 𝒰, 𝒫 and ≺ have no basis; without non-equivalence, there is nothing to retain. |
| 4. New emergent properties | Retained distinctions can constrain admissible successor structure. This is causal only in a precursor/counterfactual sense; physical causation is not yet claimed. |
| 5. Claim types | The chapter is primarily relational and functional. It states what retention, dependency, and identity criteria would have to do, not what physical substrate realizes them. |
| 6. New primitives | None is claimed beyond the two commitments so far. This remains conditional on later success in deriving physically relevant transformation families and frames rather than stipulating them. |
| 7. Fewer assumptions? | W and F are kept distinct because collapsing resolution and identity into one structure would smuggle observation into identity. Pres<sub>W</sub> is kept distinct from Ret(A<sub>F</sub>; ·) to avoid semantic collision with Return. |
| 8. Observational constraints | The construction must reproduce downstream uses of frame-relative resolution, 𝒫, ≺ and ~<sub>F</sub> without reintroducing Ω-as-anchor, physical time, or orientation prematurely. |
