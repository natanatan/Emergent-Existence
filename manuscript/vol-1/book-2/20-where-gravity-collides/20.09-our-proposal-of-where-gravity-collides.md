# 20.9 Our Proposal of Where Gravity Collides

This chapter adopts Penrose's direction and supplies a mechanism for it from the framework built so far.

Consider the moment a spread-out field meets a detector, such as the screen in the double slit experiment. The coupling is local: at some point on the screen, the interaction transfers enough to start a small, localized excitation, a concentration of organization in one place.

Chapter 19's central idea now applies at the finest scale. A localized, retained organization conditions the relational neighborhood around it: it changes which continuations are available to the field nearby. So the new excitation reshapes the field around it. And because the field after the excitation is no longer the field before it, the excitation can draw further coupling toward itself. The result is a feedback loop:

initial coupling ⇝ local excitation ⇝ stronger local conditioning ⇝ more transfer ⇝ a retained, localized outcome

Now consider what this means for a field whose alternatives are still unresolved. Each possible place where an excitation could start would condition the surrounding field in its own way. As long as those different conditionings are small enough to be carried together within one relational neighborhood, the alternatives coexist and the field stays in superposition. This is the quantum regime, from the de Broglie scale up. But once the alternatives would shape the field into local geometries that no single neighborhood can hold together, the field can no longer carry them all. Feedback amplifies one, and that one is retained. The dot on the screen is the field settling into one geometry.

This is Penrose's mechanism read in the framework's terms. His "two incompatible spacetime geometries" are two incompatible local conditionings of the relational field. His threshold is the point at which they can no longer share a frame. And the retained outcome is what Book I called retention, now appearing as a physical event.

The chain above tells the story in the order a laboratory clock records it: emission, travel, arrival, settling. This chapter proposes that it is one event, not four, a view that two-ended accounts from Wheeler and Feynman to Cramer share (20.25). Emission, the field's passage through the apparatus, its interference and the detector's coupling form a single relational interaction. What is retained is the version of that whole interaction that is consistent at both of its ends, the emitter that sent and the detector that received. The detector's condition helps determine the interaction just as the emitter's does. Seen from inside time, that looks retrocausal, as if the receiving end shaped what was sent. But nothing travels backward, and nothing crosses the field after the dot lands. There is no second process called collapse. The settling is not an extra event added to the interaction: there is one interaction, extended in time and constrained at both of its ends, and the dot is where that interaction closes.

The framework has room for this because of what Chapter 14 earned. Time there is order recovered from retained dependency, and an interaction whose outcome has not yet been retained has no internal before and after for an influence to run against. Emission, travel and detection are the order that a retained record supports once the interaction has closed, that is, settled into the one form consistent at both of its ends. ◌ On this reading, a quantum event is a relational closure that has not yet produced its own time order, and the dot is the moment it does. This is also the simpler account. Where textbook quantum mechanics pairs a smooth law of evolution with a separate rule for measurement, which selects an outcome and fixes its probability, this proposal needs three: one extended interaction, one consistency condition across its ends, and one threshold that says when the interaction must close and be retained.

◌ The proposal goes one step further than Penrose. In Chapter 19, a retained organization conditions the relational field around it, and at large scale that conditioning appears as gravity. In this chapter, a newly formed excitation conditions the same field at the scale of a single detection, and that conditioning forces a single outcome. If these are the same self-conditioning seen at two scales, then gravity and the definiteness of measurement are not two mechanisms forced together. They are one.

That is what the chapter's title means. ◌ When interacting excitations resolve to the same relational neighborhood, their closeness measured by how their superposed patterns overlap rather than by distance in space, their combined conditioning orients the field toward a single collision. That collision is where the alternatives stop, and observation of gravitational effects is what such collisions look like when they are added up over planets and stars.
