# 12.10 Coordinates Arrive After Stable Modes

Once a family of independent modes is stable enough to be re-identified, coordinates become useful. A coordinate tuple x ↦ (x^1, …, x^n) is then a compressed label for relational position relative to those modes.

Coordinates do not produce the geometry. A different coordinate assignment may describe the same invariant relational organization. The decisive test is whether the relevant relations survive the transformation.

▪ coordinate representation ≠ geometric ontology

This gives the chapter a useful principle for later physics: if two coordinate descriptions preserve the same relational invariants, their disagreement in labels should not be mistaken for an ontological difference.

In this framework coordinates are therefore earned as efficient representations of stable mode structure, not admitted as primitive addresses in an already existing container.
