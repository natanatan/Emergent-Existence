# 12.13 Geometry as a Lossy Resolution

A bounded reference frame does not necessarily resolve every distinction present in the organization it encounters. Earlier chapters already allowed distinct underlying states to become indistinguishable to a given frame. Geometry now applies the same principle to metric organization.

Let the richer relational substrate be 𝔏. Let Π<sub>F</sub> denote the resolution map associated with a bounded frame F. The effective geometry available to that frame may be written:

▪ Π<sub>F</sub>(𝔏) = 𝔏<sub>F</sub>

Projection here does not mean that a known higher-dimensional object is being projected onto an external screen. It means that distinctions in the underlying relational organization are compressed into the subset of invariants recoverable by the frame.

This immediately permits many-to-one resolution:

▪ 𝔏<sub>a</sub> ≠ 𝔏<sub>b</sub> while Π<sub>F</sub>(𝔏<sub>a</sub>) = Π<sub>F</sub>(𝔏<sub>b</sub>)

The resulting effective geometry can still be objectively useful. Lossy does not mean fictitious. A lossy representation can preserve exactly the relations required for reliable prediction inside its domain.

▪ predictive adequacy ≠ ontological completeness
