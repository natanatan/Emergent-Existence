# 12.7 Independence Before Orthogonality

Ordinary geometric intuition makes dimension difficult to picture without right angles. That intuition comes from living inside a mature spatial representation. It should not be imported into the derivation.

To say that two modes are orthogonal normally requires a comparison rule equivalent to an inner product. In a conventional notation, one writes g(e<sub>i</sub>,e<sub>j</sub>)=0 for i ≠ j. But the object g is additional structure. Independence asks only whether one admissible mode can be reduced to the others.

The derivational order should therefore remain:

▪ independent variation ⇝ basis-like structure ⇝ comparison among modes ⇝ angle ⇝ orthogonality

The terms basis and span are representational conveniences here. The ontological proposition is thinner: a relational organization can require multiple irreducible ways of changing before any meaningful relation of ninety degrees exists among them.

This distinction becomes especially important if the first effective geometry is skewed, anisotropic, directed, or otherwise unlike Euclidean space.
