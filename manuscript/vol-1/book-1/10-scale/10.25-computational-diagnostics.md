# 10.25 Computational Diagnostics

Each test asks whether an apparent scale is relationally grounded or painted on by the analyst.

Correspondence first. Establish equal magnitude and magnitude order by bijections and injections before any integer label is assigned.

Relabeling. Change every identifier in the representation; comparative magnitude must be unchanged.

Threshold recovery. Reconstruct the Relation threshold condition from a standard collection K before replacing it with numeral shorthand.

Hierarchy against scale. Build a higher-order quotient with fewer representatives, showing that a higher level need not be a greater magnitude.

Recursive composition. Generate organizations under several recurrence rules and record their earned magnitudes.

Ratio convergence. Determine whether successive comparative ratios stabilize, cycle, stay bounded or diverge.

Recurrence perturbation. Change the coefficients and verify that φ is not generically selected.

Initial conditions. Determine when a stable ratio is robust to admissible changes in starting magnitude classes.

Self-similarity control. Compare a recursively self-similar model against a non-self-similar one that still supports magnitude comparison.

Coarse-graining sanity. Permit a scale-indexed quotient only after the standard defining it has been independently earned.

Falsification guardrail. If the scale can be recovered only once the analyst supplies a numeral, a unit, a coordinate magnitude, a metric or an iteration count, then nothing was earned: the magnitude was in the notation.
