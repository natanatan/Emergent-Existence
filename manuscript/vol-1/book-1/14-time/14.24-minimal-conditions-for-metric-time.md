# 14.24 Minimal Conditions for Metric Time

The chapter can now state a conditional route from temporal order to metric time without claiming the route is complete. A metric temporal quantity becomes defensible only if at least the following conditions can be satisfied:

ordered comparability: the relevant states admit stable precedence relations;

repeatable standard: a recurrent process provides distinguishable, countable cycles or phases;

cross-standard calibration: independently realized recurrent processes exhibit stable comparative ratios over a regime;

resolution robustness: the inferred temporal measure is not an artifact of one arbitrary decomposition into update steps;

frame coherence: overlapping local frames agree on precedence where dependency is shared, even if their quantitative clock readings later differ;

non-circularity: no external time variable or target relativistic behavior is built into the update rules that supposedly derive the measure.

These conditions do not prove that metric time must emerge. They define what the computational and mathematical program must demonstrate before the stronger claim is licensed.
