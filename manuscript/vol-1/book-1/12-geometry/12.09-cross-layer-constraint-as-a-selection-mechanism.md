# 12.9 Cross-Layer Constraint as a Selection Mechanism

Multilayer organization becomes explanatory only if the coupling among layers does work. Suppose 𝔏<sub>1</sub> admits many local variations and 𝔏<sub>2</sub> admits many others. Once the two organizations constrain one another, some combinations may remain reproducible while others destabilize or disappear under repeated transformation.

The candidate mechanism is therefore selection in relational state space:

▪ variation ⇝ cross-layer constraint ⇝ selection ⇝ retention ⇝ stable collective modes

This is not a new primitive. It is the same recursive logic already used throughout the framework: perturbation generates candidate configurations; constraint eliminates or suppresses some possibilities; retained configurations become available to later organization.

If that process repeatedly stabilizes a small number of collective modes, then effective dimensionality could be lower than the number of microscopic distinctions participating in the substrate. In schematic form, d<sub>eff</sub> may be much smaller than d<sub>micro</sub>.

▪ d<sub>eff</sub> ≪ d<sub>micro</sub> (candidate possibility)

The inequality expresses only the possibility that a low-dimensional effective geometry can be a compressed description of a much richer relational organization.
