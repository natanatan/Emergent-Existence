# 8.4 Persistence, Departure and Return Are Different

A system that never leaves its identity class has persisted; it has not necessarily returned. Otherwise every stable object would count as continuously returning to itself.

▪ Persistence: Σ₀ ~<sub>F</sub> Σ₁ ~<sub>F</sub> Σ₂ ~<sub>F</sub> …

▪ Return: Σ<sub>A</sub> ≺ₒ Σ<sub>¬F</sub> ≺ₒ Σ<sub>R</sub>, with Σ<sub>A</sub> ∈ O<sub>F</sub>, Σ<sub>¬F</sub> ∉ O<sub>F</sub>, and Σ<sub>R</sub> ∈ O<sub>F</sub>

Meaningful return therefore requires a genuine departure relative to the tested identity criterion. At least one intermediate configuration must fail that criterion:

▪ ∃ i : Σ<sub>i</sub> ∉ O<sub>F</sub>, while F remains applicable

Departure need not mean destruction of every lower-order component. It means only that the organization used to define this particular anchor is not satisfied during part of the sequence.

Two different situations hide inside that requirement and should be separated. The criterion F concerns particular relations and roles. It can be evaluated on a configuration only while those relata and roles remain available to the criterion.

▪ departure: the relata F concerns persist, but the organization does not satisfy F; the criterion is applicable and false

▪ dissolution: the relata or roles F concerns are themselves unavailable, so the criterion has no subject and is inapplicable rather than false

Return as defined here requires the first and weaker case. A sequence departs when its anchor’s criterion remains a meaningful question with a negative answer. It returns when that same question again has a positive one. The folded structure below is a departure in this sense. The parts persist, the extended organization does not hold, and the criterion stays askable throughout.

Dissolution is a stronger condition and deserves a different name. If an organization disperses so completely that its identity criterion ceases to apply. Some later configuration nevertheless satisfies that criterion again, what has occurred is re-instantiation rather than return. The distinction is not idle: whether a dissolved identity can be reconstructed at all is the question underneath death and recovery, phase change, reconstruction from records and substrate replacement. we do not settle it, and the Register carries it as open.

▪ Return requires departure under a criterion that remains applicable throughout. Open question: whether a dissolved identity can be reconstructed, and whether such re-instantiation is a stronger form of return or a different relation altogether.

A deployable structure may fold into a configuration that no longer performs its extended structural role and later unfold into a functionally equivalent one. Its material may remain continuously present while the higher-order identity criterion under examination is left and later recovered.
