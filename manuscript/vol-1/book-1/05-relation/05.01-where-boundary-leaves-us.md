# 5.1 Where Boundary Leaves Us

A single retained distinction is still too small to look like a world. Relation widens the frame until several retained relations are visible at once.

At first they may be only a collection. Then one relation begins to limit what another can be. The scene changes: we are no longer cataloguing differences; we are watching organization appear among them.

Our task is to locate that transition precisely.

The question mark is the burden of this chapter. Relation becomes structure only if the multiplication of relations produces organization that is not exhausted by listing them separately.

▪ Boundary supplies retained distinction and non-spatial boundary. Open question: whether several retained relations can generate integrated organization without a new primitive.
