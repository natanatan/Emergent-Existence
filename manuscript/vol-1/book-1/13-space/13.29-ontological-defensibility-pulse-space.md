# 13.29 Ontological Defensibility Pulse: Space

> This is the chapter’s Pulse sidebar. It applies to the central proposition, and it is set apart from the argument so that a reader can skip it on a first pass.

| **Pulse question** | **Applied to Space** |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Entities required | Effective geometric loci, relational identities, metric neighborhoods, localization profiles and admissible relational couplings. No spatial substance and no external container |
| 2. Ontological level | Higher-order effective organization of already-earned geometry. Spatiality belongs to coordinated localization rather than to primitive relations |
| 3. Lower-order dependencies | Distinction, retained identity, Relation, Closure, Orientation, Return, Formalism, Scale, Distance and Geometry |
| 4. New emergent properties | Mutual localization constraint, consequential adjacency, locality, extension, spatial dimensionality, and possibly responsive geometric organization |
| 5. Claim types | Relational and functional at the core; formal where localization and coordination can be specified; speculative on responsive geometry and on unresolved fine structure |
| 6. New primitives | Ideally none. The principal danger is an admissible-coupling rule that already contains spatial locality |
| 7. Fewer assumptions? | Strongest where locality and extension follow from inherited relational dynamics; weakened wherever adjacency, dimension, continuity or coordinates must be inserted separately |
| 8. Observational constraints | The seven tests of 13.26: coordinate removal, locality prediction against randomized baselines, perturbation, probe heterogeneity, scale robustness, smoothness failure and responsive geometry |

> The principal vulnerability is best stated on its own, since everything in the chapter depends on the answer.

> Does spatial coordination emerge from the relational organization, or has spatial locality been reintroduced through the coupling rule?

> If that question cannot be answered, the chapter has not earned Space.
