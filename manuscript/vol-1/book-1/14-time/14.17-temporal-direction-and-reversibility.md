# 14.17 Temporal Direction and Reversibility

Asymmetric dependency does not imply that every microscopic transformation is dynamically irreversible. A reversible law can relate two states symmetrically while the realized history contains records or retained consequences that distinguish one dependency orientation from the other.

The chapter therefore separates three questions:

Is the dependency relation asymmetric?

Are the transformation rules dynamically reversible?

Does a higher-order statistical arrow arise from the distribution of retained records and accessible states?

Only the first is directly addressed here. The second belongs to the eventual physical dynamics. The third invokes entropy, coarse-graining, boundary conditions, and statistical mechanics, none of which Book I has yet earned.
