# 20.3 What Has Been Measured

The following results bind any viable account of light, including ours.

-   **Speed.** In empty space light travels at 299,792,458 metres per second (m/s). Since 1983 that number is exact, because the metre is defined from it. Every measurement made in any direction, and by observers in uniform motion relative to one another, gives the same value (Michelson and Morley 1887 onward).

-   **No dependence on frequency.** Gamma rays of very different energies from one distant burst (GRB 090510) arrived within about a second of each other after travelling for billions of years†. Within that precision, every frequency moves at the same speed.

-   **Frequency and wavelength.** For every frequency f (in hertz, Hz, cycles per second) and wavelength λ (in metres), the product fλ equals the speed of light, c.

-   **Two orientations across the travel.** We call the ideal case the **free-propagation regime**: a locally charge-free region sufficiently far from material boundaries and localized sources, outside dominant near-field structure, approximately homogeneous, and without strong imposed anisotropy. Under those conditions the propagating electromagnetic field is transverse to the direction of travel and supports two independent polarization degrees of freedom. A filter at angle θ to the light's orientation passes the fraction cos²θ (Malus 1809).

-   **Energy delivered in units.** When light is absorbed, the energy delivered per absorption event is hf, where h = 6.626 070 15 × 10⁻³⁴ joule-seconds (J·s), exact by definition since 2019. Below a threshold frequency a metal ejects no electrons however bright the light (Millikan 1916).

-   **Forward push.** Absorbed light delivers momentum equal to its energy divided by c (radiation pressure, measured by Nichols and Hull and by Lebedev around 1900†); each scattering event delivers h/λ (Compton 1923).

-   **Rotational push.** Circularly polarized light delivers angular momentum in the ratio energy ÷ angular frequency, which per absorbed unit is ±ħ = ±1.054 571 817 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s at every frequency (Beth 1936†).

-   **Interference and spreading.** Overlapping light produces stable bright and dark bands (Young 1802). A beam of width D spreads at an angle of about λ/D.

-   **Light ordinarily passes through light.** In weak-field ordinary propagation, two light beams cross with negligible interaction and continue essentially unchanged. High-energy or sufficiently intense conditions can produce light-by-light scattering, registered by the ATLAS experiment at CERN in 2017 (20.23).

-   **Energy balances.** In every measured emission, scattering and absorption, the energy given up equals the energy received.

-   **Every registration is an interaction.** No measurement has registered light's energy without an interaction with something that absorbs or scatters it.
