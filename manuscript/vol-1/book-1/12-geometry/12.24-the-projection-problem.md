# 12.24 The Projection Problem

The language of projection creates an immediate danger. If every inconvenient observation can be declared a lossy projection of an invisible substrate, the hypothesis cannot fail and therefore explains nothing.

A legitimate projection model must independently constrain three things: the candidate substrate 𝔏, the interaction I<sub>F</sub>, and the resolution map Π<sub>F</sub>. These cannot all be chosen after seeing the output that the model is supposed to explain.

▪ 𝔏 + independently constrained interaction rules + independently constrained resolution ⇝ predicted effective geometry

The strongest test is generative. Begin with admissible lower-order relations that contain no target coordinates or preferred dimensionality. Let the system organize under independently specified constraints. Then measure the stable effective rank and metric relations using probes that were not tuned to return the desired answer.

If the analyst must insert three-dimensional adjacency, Euclidean neighborhoods, or the target embedding into the update rules, the model has reproduced the conclusion by construction rather than earned it.
