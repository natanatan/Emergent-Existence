# 14.25 Candidate Diagnostics and Failure Tests

A chapter on emergent time should be falsifiable at the level of its proposed mechanism. The following tests are therefore more important than rhetorical resemblance to familiar temporal concepts.

Index-removal test. Remove all external iteration counters, timestamps, and manuscript-order labels. Determine whether the precedence relation can still be reconstructed from retained relational structure.

Counterfactual-dependency test. Delete or perturb a purported predecessor and test whether the admissibility or structure of the claimed successor changes in the predicted way.

Cycle-calibration test. Construct at least two independently organized recurrent subsystems and test whether their comparative cycle ratios stabilize without one clock being defined from the other.

Coarse-graining test. Change temporal resolution and determine whether precedence and any proposed metric ratios remain stable over a finite regime.

Branching test. Introduce incomparable or branching update structures and verify that the formalism does not force an artificial global total order.

Frame-overlap test. Give neighboring local frames partial access to the same dependency network and test whether they recover compatible orderings on the shared portion.

Reverse-rule test. Use transformation laws that are microscopically reversible and determine whether the framework incorrectly manufactures thermodynamic irreversibility from dependency notation alone.

Spacetime-withholding test. Verify that no propagation speed, Lorentzian signature, null relation, or invariant c is required to make the temporal construction work.

A candidate model that fails the index-removal or counterfactual-dependency tests has not derived temporal order. A model that passes those but fails cross-clock calibration may have earned succession without metric time. That would be a meaningful partial result rather than a disguised success.
