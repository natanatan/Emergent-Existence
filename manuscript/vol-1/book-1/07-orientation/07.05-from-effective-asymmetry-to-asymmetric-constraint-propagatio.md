# 7.5 From Effective Asymmetry to Asymmetric Constraint Propagation

An effective asymmetry is still not orientation. It becomes orientational when it enters the dependency architecture. Suppose a change in r<sub>i</sub> changes the admissible states of r<sub>j</sub> while the reversed dependency is not structurally equivalent:

▪ Δr<sub>i</sub> ⇒ Δ𝒫(r<sub>j</sub>), while Δr<sub>j</sub> ⇏ an equivalent Δ𝒫(r<sub>i</sub>)

This is stronger than unequal labels and weaker than physical causation. It says the relational organization distinguishes two dependency roles because exchanging them changes admissible consequences. At the simplest level this justifies the orientational relation ri ≺ₒ rj. The richer object is the whole asymmetric constraint structure K = \[K<sub>ij</sub>\], which may branch, converge, overlap, or support several non-redundant orientational modes.
