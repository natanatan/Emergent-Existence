# 12.15 Measurement Is an Internal Relation

The projection map becomes more subtle when the frame must actively probe the organization it seeks to resolve. At sufficiently coarse scales, the influence of the measuring system may be negligible relative to the stable structure being measured. At finer scales that approximation need not hold.

A probe is itself a bounded relational system. It is made of the same reality it interrogates. Measurement should therefore be represented as an interaction within the substrate rather than as passive inspection from outside it.

Write I<sub>F</sub> for the interaction induced by frame F. A more complete schematic relation is:

▪ (𝔏,F) –I<sub>F</sub>–\> G<sub>prime</sub> –Π<sub>F</sub>–\> O<sub>F</sub>

where G<sub>prime</sub> is the post-interaction relational organization and O<sub>F</sub> is the resolved observable. Equivalently:

▪ O<sub>F</sub> = Π<sub>F</sub>( I<sub>F</sub>(𝔏) )

This separates two forms of information loss. Resolution loss occurs when several substrate distinctions map to the same effective result. Measurement back-action occurs when the coupling needed to resolve the system changes the relation being resolved. They may coexist, but they should not be treated as the same phenomenon.

The chapter does not identify this generic structure with any particular interpretation of quantum measurement. It establishes only the more general possibility that fine-scale observables may be interaction-conditioned projections rather than transparent copies of pre-existing classical properties.
