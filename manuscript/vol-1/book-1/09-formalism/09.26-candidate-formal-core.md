# 9.26 Candidate Formal Core

The results so far support a deliberately weak core, 𝔊 = (𝒞, ℜ, 𝒰), with:

𝒞, admissible relational configurations, with no assumed metric, topology, vector structure or probability measure.

ℜ, the family of earned relations, not restricted in advance to one arity.

𝒰 ⊆ 𝒞 × 𝒞, the admissible transition relation.

~<sub>F</sub>, inherited structural equivalence induced by declared identity criteria.

𝔗, declared transformation and test classes used to state invariants.

Derived predicates and structures: PresW and retained r<sub>ij</sub>; reachability 𝒫; retained-dependency precedence ≺; identity equivalence ~<sub>F</sub> with O<sub>F</sub> = \[Σ\]<sub>~F</sub>; Closed<sub>F</sub>; representative-independent higher-order addressability; organizational anchor A<sub>F</sub>; oriented dependency ≺ₒ and orientation structure 𝔒; and Return, written Ret(A<sub>F</sub>;·).

The core is compatible with several concrete encodings. Graphs, hypergraphs, transition systems, relation algebras, simplicial complexes and categorical descriptions may each capture part of the same content. The ontological commitment belongs to what survives faithful changes of encoding, not to the machinery of one notation.

▪ representation-invariant relational content = candidate ontological commitment

This is representational pluralism with a minimal common core, not formal relativism. Different encodings are admissible only insofar as they preserve the earned structure.
