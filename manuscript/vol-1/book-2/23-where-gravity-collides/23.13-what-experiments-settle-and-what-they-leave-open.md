# 23.13 What Experiments Settle, and What They Leave Open

A careful reader will want to know how much of this chapter's account experiments have established, and how much is this framework's reading of them.

An experiment does not hand us a mechanism. It gives us records. A team prepares a system, arranges an apparatus, couples a detector, and counts outcomes over many trials. What the records establish is a distribution of outcomes conditional on the arrangement, which can be written P(Z | X, Y, …): the probability of outcome Z given the controlled conditions X, Y and the rest. Behind that expression sit calibration, background subtraction, detector response and the statistical model against which significance is judged. A five-sigma result means that a specified null model is very unlikely to have produced the data under those assumptions. It does not mean that every term in a successfully predictive theory names something observed.

So the strength of evidence for an effect and the strength of evidence for one story about the effect are different things. The double slit experiment establishes the stripes, the dots and the loss of stripes when a slit detector is in place, with overwhelming confidence. It does not establish that a photon traveled a path, or that a wave collapsed. Those are interpretations, and several interpretations reproduce the same records.

That gives the rule this chapter follows, stated in the Methodology for every physical chapter: preserve what experiments establish, and reopen what they leave underdetermined. An experiment can choose between two accounts only where the accounts predict different records. If two accounts O₁ and O₂ give the same distribution in experiment E, then E supports what they share and is silent on where they differ. Discrimination begins only at an experiment E₂ where their predictions part.

Standard quantum mechanics is the most precisely confirmed theory in science, and every one of its confirmed predictions is a target this framework must meet. Its vocabulary of particles, paths and collapse is not, by itself, such a target. The account so far used that freedom. The sections that follow have to pay for it, by showing that the framework's account reaches the same records and, eventually, by finding an experiment where it predicts different ones.
