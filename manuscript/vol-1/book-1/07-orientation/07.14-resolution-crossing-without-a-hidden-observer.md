# 7.14 Resolution Crossing Without a Hidden Observer

A predictable objection is that “resolution” makes orientation observer-dependent. The reply distinguishes an external epistemic resolving map from an internally realized higher-order equivalence. An external modeler may describe a coarse-graining with W⁽ᴸ⁾, but the ontic counterpart comes from Closure. A higher-order identity groups lower-order realizations according to an invariant F. If a lower-order asymmetry makes no difference to F or to any admissible higher-order relation, it is effectively invisible to that identity. If recursive organization makes the asymmetry alter F-relevant dependency or return, it has become internally consequential whether or not anyone notices.

▪ observer coarse-graining ≠ ontic higher-order equivalence, though the former may model the latter

This is also why Origin’s resolution-relative formulation does not become epistemic idealism. The theory does not claim reality is symmetric because an observer cannot see deeper. It claims only that the initial ontology should not assert more absence of distinction than its admitted resolving frame can justify. Boundary generalizes the same restraint to later resolving frames.
