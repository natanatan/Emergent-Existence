# 1.11 Relation Before Geometry

The ascent will fail immediately if the first relation already smuggles in space.

So one further constraint belongs in the opening chapter.

A relation can be formally specified without assigning coordinates, lengths, angles, or a spatial embedding. That mathematical fact does not prove that physical reality is relational at bottom, but it shows that relation does not logically require geometry.

This volume therefore treats geometry as a target rather than a background.

Any primitive relation introduced in Difference must avoid silently meaning near, far, inside, outside, earlier, later, toward, away, or adjacent in physical space.

Likewise, recursion may establish an order of derivation without yet establishing duration. Orientation may later distinguish relational roles without becoming a spatial axis. Return may later re-establish identity without becoming a geometric loop.

These separations are not semantic fussiness. They are what make the volume’s final question non-circular:

Can a sufficiently organized relational system earn geometry without geometry having been assumed in the relations used to construct it?

Book I carries the relational derivation through Time. Book II begins where the separately earned structures of Space and Time must be shown to support a physically instantiated spacetime and the dynamics associated with it. Geometry, Space and Time are therefore targets of the prephysical derivation rather than evidence that physical spacetime has already been obtained.

Whether an earned geometry can acquire the dynamical structure of physical spacetime belongs on the far side of the volume. That later test remains essential, but it is no longer a promise Origin needs to front-load.
