# 23.22 Candidate Diagnostics and Failure Tests

The chapter's central mechanism should be revised or rejected if any of the following holds.

- The relational field cannot generate a phase-like variable, and an interference law with a cross term, from cycles and transformations already earned.
- The derived interference is not second order: it produces genuine three-route terms, which experiment excludes.
- The localization mechanism needs a particle trajectory, a collapse rule or the Born rule inserted before they have been derived.
- A local excitation does not change the available dynamics of the field enough to produce stable localization.
- The feedback violates energy conservation, or requires the unretained part of the field simply to disappear.
- No relational dynamics yields detector-speed retention.
- The distribution of retained outcomes cannot be derived, or does not match the Born frequencies.
- ◌ When single emissions are timed against the cycle of the field driving the emitter, that timing shows no link to which stripe each dot lands in. The phase-steering proposal of 23.17 predicts such a link; standard quantum mechanics predicts none. The test needs the phase of each emission, measured against the detector's answering wave, to be known or set. Timing arrivals cannot yet supply it. Visible light completes a cycle in about 2 femtoseconds, far faster than the few picoseconds to which the best single-photon detectors can time an arrival; in the microwave range a 5 gigahertz field cycles every 200 picoseconds, but today's single-photon detectors there time arrivals only to tens of nanoseconds. Designing an arrangement that fixes the phase at both ends is part of the burden.
- Coupling a detector at the slits reproduces the loss of interference only because the coupling rule assumes it.
- The incompatibility measure cannot be defined independently of the outcome it is meant to explain.
- The threshold lies in a region already excluded by collapse-model experiments, or predicts heating or radiation above measured bounds.
- The relational account of entanglement exceeds Tsirelson's bound, falls short of quantum correlations, or permits signaling.
- Disordered locality breaks Lorentz invariance beyond observed bounds.
- No mathematical consistency condition across an interaction's two ends can be stated that selects exactly one retained outcome.
- The two-ended interaction permits controllable signaling into the past, or changes a record already made in a delayed-choice arrangement.
- The account's loss of measurement independence lets the source's state predict or control the detector settings, or explains correlations beyond the quantum ones.
- The large-scale expression of the conditioning fails to propagate at the speed of light.
- The same dynamics cannot recover a single smooth geometry when alternatives stop mattering.
- The gravitational connection reduces to ordinary weak gravity, with no viable route to the coupling hierarchy.
- The account matches existing results only because experimental parameters or target distributions are written into its update rules.
- No experiment can be identified in which the relational account and a competing account predict different records.

These tests make the chapter deliberately vulnerable. A relational vocabulary carries no evidential weight of its own. If the mechanism cannot derive new constraints, compress existing ones, or eventually predict something its competitors do not, it is an interpretation of known physics, not a new physical account.
