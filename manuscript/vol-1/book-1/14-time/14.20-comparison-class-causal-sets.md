# 14.20 Comparison Class: Causal Sets

Causal-set theory provides an important mathematical comparison because it treats a locally finite partial order as fundamental and seeks to recover continuum spacetime from order plus discreteness. The present framework should not be identified with causal-set theory: its dependency relation is derived from retained relational consequence rather than postulated as a causal order, and local finiteness has not been assumed as a primitive.

If the Time construction is viable, one should ask whether the quotient order (𝓔/≈≺ , ≺ₜ) can satisfy or approximate the structural conditions from which causal-set approaches recover Lorentzian manifold behavior. Failure here would be informative: it could mean that retained dependency is too weak, that additional constraints are required, or that the physical extension of the framework is wrong.

The crucial discipline is that causal-set results may serve as diagnostics or comparison targets. They cannot be imported as proof that ≺ is already a relativistic causal relation.
