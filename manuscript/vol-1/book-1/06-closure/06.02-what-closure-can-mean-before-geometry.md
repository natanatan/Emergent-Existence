# 6.2 What Closure Can Mean Before Geometry

Ordinary language makes closure sound spatial. A door closes, a curve closes, a membrane encloses, a surface separates an interior from an exterior. None of those senses is primitive here. No coordinate chart, metric, adjacency relation, angle, volume, or spatial side has yet been derived.

The first definition must therefore be relational. Let ℛ be a relational domain whose constituent relations mutually constrain one another. Let F denote an organization-level invariant of the kind introduced in 5.6. F need not yet be a number. It is whatever relational organization must survive for the domain to remain the same higher-order structure under the transformations presently admitted.

Working definition. A relational domain ℛ is structurally closed with respect to F when every dependency constitutively required to maintain F is supplied within ℛ, except for inputs explicitly classified as non-constitutive conditions rather than parts of F itself.

The qualification matters. Closure is not absolute independence. A later physical or biological system can exchange material, energy or information with an environment while retaining an internally organized identity. Theoretical biology makes this distinction explicitly: Montévil and Mossio characterize biological organization through mutually dependent constraints while simultaneously treating organisms as thermodynamically open systems (Montévil and Mossio, 2015). Their proposal operates at a much richer physical and biological level than this chapter. It supplies a useful warning: organizational closure and environmental isolation are not the same claim.

▪ closure with respect to F ≠ isolation from all external relation

At this stage, “internal” and “external” are not spatial words. A dependency is internal when it belongs to the relational organization whose persistence constitutes F. A dependency is external when the organization requires it but it does not participate in that organization. Spatial interior and exterior remain unearned.
