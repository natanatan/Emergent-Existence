# 9.28 Computational Diagnostics

A computational implementation may use richer mathematics than our ontology, subject to 9.3. The following checks should be routine.

Representation invariance. Relabel states or use an alternative faithful encoding; claimed ontological results should survive.

Transition typing. Verify whether 𝒰 is relational, functional, total, partial, deterministic or branching rather than assuming one case.

Compatibility test. Check whether the chosen F-equivalence supports a well-defined quotient transition.

Quotient return. Test whether higher-order recurrence can occur without lower-order state repetition.

Coarse-graining determinism test. Determine whether determinism is preserved, created or destroyed by the quotient.

Behavioral-equivalence comparison. Compare F-equivalence with a stronger bisimulation-like criterion.

Higher-arity test. Compare binary encoding with an n-ary or hypergraph representation and check whether invariants change.

Relations-among-relations test. Reify lower-order relations as higher-order relata and verify which structural properties survive.

Simplicial realization. Where a simplicial representation is used, verify boundary identities such as ∂² = 0 without reading them as foundational closure.

Finite-order recurrence reduction. Verify the equivalence between a k-th order recurrence and a first-order update on an enlarged state.

Independence test. Explore whether a matroid-like independence system reproduces the pre-vector-space notion of d<sub>R</sub>.

Falsification guardrail. If a formal result depends on structure that exists only because the chosen representation inserted it, whether coordinates, metric, directed edges, linear combinations, probabilities or target cycles, the formalism has exposed an unearned assumption rather than derived an ontological property.
