# 13.5 Localization Is a Relational Role

Ordinary language treats location as though an object first exists and is then assigned a position. The present framework reverses that dependence. A spatial location is not primitively possessed. It is constituted by relations.

Let λ<sub>Geo</sub>(A) denote the geometric relational profile through which A is situated within Geo. Then define a candidate localization class:

▪ Loc(A) = \[λ<sub>Geo</sub>(A)\]<sub>~Sp</sub>

where ~Sp identifies lower-order realizations that preserve the geometric profile through which A is situated. Calling those relations spatially relevant would be premature here, since spatial relevance is precisely what 13.6 goes on to define: they become spatial once they are mutually coordinated. A location can therefore remain stable even while its microscopic realization changes.

▪ A’ ≠ A, while Loc(A’) ~ Loc(A)

This continues an argument already used repeatedly throughout the framework: higher-order identity does not require microscopic identity of every constituent. Now that principle applies to position itself.
