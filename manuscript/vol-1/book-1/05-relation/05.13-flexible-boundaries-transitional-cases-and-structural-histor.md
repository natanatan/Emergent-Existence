# 5.13 Flexible Boundaries, Transitional Cases and Structural History

A higher-order boundary need not preserve exactly the same membership across every admissible configuration. What can persist is the criterion F together with a pattern of constitutive participation, while individual relations enter, leave, or change realization. The appropriate precursor to history here is retained-dependency precedence, not an assumed interval of duration.

▪ Σa ≺ Σb when retained contribution from Σa changes the admissibility of Σb

Along an admissible succession, the relations constitutive of F may change while the higher-order criterion remains satisfied. A transitional case is simply one in which the current organization does not yet satisfy the same participation pattern as either of two nearby identity-relevant regimes.

▪ boundary stability ≠ boundary rigidity

The criterion by which membership is resolved can persist while particular members enter, leave or remain ambiguous. Higher-order identity is therefore compatible with change in composition.

A stronger case occurs when two configurations that are equivalent under the present coarse description nevertheless have different admissible futures because their retained-dependency histories differ. That is path dependence without requiring metric thresholds or elapsed time. It is not yet memory: memory in the stronger biological sense waits until retained change becomes internally consequential to a self-maintaining system. Relation earns only the possibility that retained history can constrain present organization.

▪ Persistence of an identity criterion does not require invariant membership. Retained-dependency path dependence is a structural precursor to stronger notions of history and memory without yet supplying physical time.
