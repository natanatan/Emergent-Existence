# 14.2 What Time Must Not Presuppose

Several ordinary words become dangerous here because each can import the target structure before it has been earned. “Earlier,” “later,” “during,” “rate,” “delay,” and “propagation” are useful once temporal structure exists, but they cannot serve as primitives in the derivation. The chapter therefore separates five orders that are often collapsed in ordinary language:

logical order: one statement or condition is required to formulate another;

derivational order: one element of the framework is constructed before another in the argument;

dependency order: one relational state contains retained consequences of another;

temporal order: dependency structure is stably interpretable as succession among transformations;

metric time: temporal separation admits a reproducible quantitative standard.

Only the first three are available at the start of this chapter, and even dependency order is available only in Boundary’s deliberately weak sense. The task is to determine when additional structural conditions warrant a temporal ordering and what further conditions would be required for metric time.

▪ logical order ⇝ derivational order ⇝ retained dependency ≺ ⇝ derived temporal order ⇝ metric time

The arrows in this hierarchy indicate candidate dependence, not elapsed duration. Even the diagram must not be read as a clock.
