# 1.9 The Starting Claim and the Two Commitments

Everything later in the book rests on two propositions stated here: a claim about what any observable reality requires, and the two working commitments from which the construction begins.

The first is the starting claim, and it concerns persistence.

Proposition 1A - retained distinction. An observable relational difference must be able to survive transformation long enough to constrain later states.

distinction ⇝ persistence ⇝ retained distinction ⇝ information

This separation is essential. Difference is not automatically information. The later concept of a relational bit, rᵢⱼ, depends on a distinction becoming persistent enough to remain resolvable and consequential.

The second names the commitments themselves.

Proposition 1B - two working commitments. The framework provisionally treats difference, a bare non-equivalence prior to measure, direction or observer, as the first commitment from which relational differentiation begins, and admissible transformation, a constraint on what may follow what, as the second. Distinction is the later achievement by which difference becomes operative in that succession.

This proposition remains provisional because Origin does not explain why difference is admitted at all.

The open problem should remain visible:

Why does differentiation occur at all?

Several possibilities may later compete: differentiation could be logically unavoidable, dynamically favored, unstable to suppress, or simply the primitive fact from which explanation begins. The current framework has not earned a verdict among them.

Without an alternative precursor, we accept difference as a possible foundational premise, given a world in which innumerable distinctions arguably exist. For now, the working strategy is empirical in the broad methodological sense: accept the minimum primitive provisionally, then test how much structure can be built before additional primitives become necessary.
