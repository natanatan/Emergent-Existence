# 6.17 Computational Diagnostics of Closure

A conceptual definition becomes more useful when it can constrain a computational test. The computational companion can operationalize closure without promoting its readouts into ontology. This distinction is essential: an external simulation may count nodes, edges, failures and trials even when cardinality has not yet been derived as an internal primitive of the modeled world.

Four diagnostics are natural.

| **Diagnostic** | **Computational readout** | **What it tests** | **What it does not establish** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dependency leakage | Number or fraction of F-maintaining dependencies that terminate outside the candidate domain | Internal sufficiency | That numerical ratio is fundamental ontology |
| Constraint participation | Ablation sensitivity of candidate constitutive relations compared with non-constitutive controls | Whether listed relations actually matter to persistence | That all causal structure has been derived |
| Closure robustness | Fraction of preregistered perturbations in 𝔗C after which Closed<sub>F</sub> remains true | Persistence beyond one trajectory | That nature selects the tested rule |
| Higher-order addressability | Agreement of macro-outcomes across distinct microstates in the same F-equivalence class under the same external coupling | Whether an external relation descends consistently to the higher-order unit | That the higher-order relation is yet physical causation |

A fifth diagnostic can compare candidate closures against matched random, merely connected, or equally dense control subgraphs. The key question is whether the closure criterion identifies a nontrivial class whose persistence and higher-order addressability exceed those controls without having the target grouping supplied in advance. The strongest simulation guardrail remains the same as elsewhere in the project. If closure appears only because the target boundary or topology is hard-coded, closure has not been generated.
