# 10.24 Worked Example: Number After Relation

A small exercise makes the order of discovery explicit. Take three organizations, A = {a, b, c}, B = {x, y, z} and C = {u, v, w, q}. The braces are external notation; what our model requires is only that the listed members be distinguishable under the declared criterion.

First, pair A with B: a↔x, b↔y, c↔z. Nothing is left unmatched, so A ~# B. We know the two have the same cardinal magnitude without having named it.

Now inject A into C: a↦u, b↦v, c↦w. The member q is unmatched, so for these finite collections C has the greater cardinal magnitude.

Only now do the conventional labels enter: mag(A) = mag(B) = 3, mag(C) = 4. The order is the lesson, and it is the whole chapter in miniature: number arrives after relation.

The same logic carries into recursion. If a rule builds M<sub>n+1</sub> as the disjoint composition of M<sub>n</sub> and M<sub>n-1</sub>, then once cardinal magnitude is available, mag(M<sub>n+1</sub>) = mag(M<sub>n</sub>) + mag(M<sub>n-1</sub>). Fibonacci growth follows from that particular building. The building remains a model choice unless something independent motivates it.
