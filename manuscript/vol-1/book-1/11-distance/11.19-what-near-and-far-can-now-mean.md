# 11.19 What Near and Far Can Now Mean

Ordinary language may finally begin to return. If d(A,B)\<d(A,C), then B is relationally nearer to A than C is under the declared metric regime. If d(A,B)\>d(A,C), it is farther.

These words now compress something the framework has actually earned. They no longer mean merely that the analyst drew nodes closer together on the page.

▪ near/far = comparative metric relations

▪ relational near/far ≠ physical spatial near/far
