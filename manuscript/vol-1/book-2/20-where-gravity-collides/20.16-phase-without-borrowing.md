# 20.16 Phase Without Borrowing

20.6 proposed that phase is position within the cycle earned in Return. That proposal carries two burdens.

The first is to produce phase without importing it. Quantum mechanics represents amplitudes as complex numbers, and it would be easy to define a relational variable that behaves like a complex amplitude simply because the answer is known. The framework must instead show that relative position in a Return cycle combines the way phase does, with the cross term arising from the dynamics rather than by stipulation. A schematic form,

C_ab = C_a + C_b + I(Δθ_ab)

where C_a and C_b are the contributions of two parts, Δθ_ab is the difference in their cycle positions and I is the interference term, is only a target. The form of I must come from the relational dynamics. If it is chosen because it matches the quantum result, nothing has been derived.

The second burden is that phase alone gives ordinary wave interference, which water waves and radio waves also show. Quantum interference has further, sharply tested properties. Each detection follows the squared amplitude of the Born rule (20.17), which is also why adding a third slit adds nothing beyond the pairwise terms, as experiments have confirmed to about one part in a hundred (Sinha and colleagues, 2010). And its joint states produce Bell correlations (20.20). A phase derived from Return must deliver both.

There is a second route, which this chapter records without taking. Since 2001, *reconstruction* programs (Hardy; Chiribella, D'Ariano and Perinotti; Masanes and Müller) have derived the full mathematical structure of quantum theory from a few operational principles. The principle that most sharply separates quantum from classical theory is, roughly, that any definite state can be turned into any other continuously and reversibly. That is a statement about admissible transformations, the framework's second commitment, written 𝒰 in Book I. If 𝒰 can be shown to have that property under the conditions of this chapter, the reconstruction theorems would supply the rest. The two routes are compatible, and either would turn an open burden into a concrete target.
