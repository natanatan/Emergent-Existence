# 10.26 Competing Routes

Four routes to magnitude were available. The chapter took the first for reasons worth stating rather than leaving to a table.

| **Route** | **What it offers** | **Hazard** | **Used here** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cardinality first | Equal, more and less from correspondence | Too crude for later physical magnitude | The foundational route |
| Hierarchy first | Matches recursive organization directly | Confuses level with size | Rejected as a definition |
| Resolution first | Ties magnitude to what a frame can distinguish | Makes magnitude observer-relative | Rejected; 10.19 keeps them apart |
| Metric first | Immediate and familiar | Imports distance, units and geometry wholesale | Unavailable until Distance |

Cardinality is the weakest of the four, which is our argument for it. It needs nothing but distinguishable members and pairing, both of which we have held since Distinction.
