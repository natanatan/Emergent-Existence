# 1.3 A Beginning Without a First Moment

The word “beginning” usually carries a clock. In this book it carries a ledger instead: what has been earned, and in what order.

A physical theory may ask whether the universe has a first moment, whether an earlier phase preceded our present spacetime, or whether temporal extension is finite or unbounded. Those questions have to wait. They are asked in Time, and time has not yet been earned. Origin’s question comes before them in a different sense.

If one concept cannot be specified without another, the prerequisite is logically prior even if no physical time separates them. A coordinate system is conceptually downstream of distinguishable positions. Measurement is downstream of something that can be compared. A retained difference is downstream of a difference that can exist at all.

Here we use our ledger to distinguish logical or derivational priority from physical temporal priority.

№ 1.1 derivational priority ≠ temporal priority

When a later chapter says one structure comes “before” another, read it from the ledger, not the clock: it records what must be earned first, not what happened first.

This distinction will matter repeatedly. Distinction will use recursion without treating its index as metric time. Boundary will speak of persistence without duration. Orientation will earn asymmetry without spatial direction. Return will earn re-entry without a physical period. Time will eventually ask what more is required before ordered transformation deserves a temporal reading.

For now, “beginning” means the point at which the derivational bookkeeping starts.
