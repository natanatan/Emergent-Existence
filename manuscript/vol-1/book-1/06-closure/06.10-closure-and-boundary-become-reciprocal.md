# 6.10 Closure and Boundary Become Reciprocal

Boundary introduced boundary as preserved non-equivalence before there was an inside or outside. Relation generalized membership to multi-relation domains. Closure now gives higher-order membership a stronger basis.

A relation belongs to a closed organization when it participates in the dependency structure constitutive of F. The boundary can therefore be read as the distinction between relations whose participation is required for that closure and relations that are not constitutive of it.

▪ inside<sub>F</sub> = participation in the F-maintaining closure

▪ outside<sub>F</sub> = non-participation in that closure

Our result is nevertheless stronger than Boundary’s pairwise separability. A higher-order organization can now carry an internally defined membership structure.

Closure and boundary therefore constrain one another. Boundary states which relations count as participating in the organization. Closure states why those relations count together as one organization. Neither needs to be primitive if both can be recovered from the same persistent constraint organization.

The biological closure-of-constraints literature again provides a useful analogue rather than evidence. Montévil and Mossio argue that organizational closure can help mark boundaries between interacting biological systems. Hordijk and Steel show the complementary point that a self-sustaining network formalism need not contain a boundary unless boundary relations are represented. Together, those cases warn against assuming either direction of implication without specifying the mechanism.
