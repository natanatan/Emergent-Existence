# 6.8 Perturbation and the Admissible Transformation Class

Endogenous persistence is not the same as robustness. A structure may remain closed along the one trajectory it happens to follow while collapsing under the slightest alternative perturbation. Boundary introduced a diagnostic transformation family distinct from internal succession, and Relation specialized it for organization-level invariance. Closure can now sharpen that same methodological role rather than introduce another transformation primitive.

𝒰 and the diagnostic family 𝔗C should remain distinct. 𝒰 belongs to the modeled succession structure. 𝔗C is a declared robustness test, chosen before the closure result is inspected. A candidate transformation belongs to 𝔗C only if it can be described using structures already earned at this ontological level and does not insert the very property being tested.

At minimum, a defensible perturbation class should satisfy four constraints:

Type preservation. The transformation maps admissible relational configurations to admissible relational configurations. It does not add coordinates, metric, physical time or other later structure.

Nontriviality. 𝔗C contains at least one transformation other than identity or pure relabeling.

Pre-specification. The class is stated before the closure result is inspected, preventing F and 𝔗C from being tuned after the fact to protect any desired grouping.

Relevance. The transformations challenge relations that plausibly matter to the candidate organization rather than only changing inert labels.

Examples at the present level include permutation of labels, deletion or substitution of a retained relation, replacement of one lower-order realization by another relation-preserving realization. Alternative state updates that remain within the permitted relational formalism.

A closure robust across 𝔗C is stronger than one preserved only by its actual admissible trajectory. Dynamic persistence asks what occurs along succession; robustness asks which declared variations preserve the organization.
