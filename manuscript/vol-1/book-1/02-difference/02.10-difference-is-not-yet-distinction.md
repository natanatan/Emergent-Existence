# 2.10 Difference Is Not Yet Distinction

The chapter’s most important stopping point is negative. A structure can be non-equivalent without containing any internally admissible operation that makes use of that non-equivalence. The theorist can describe a ≬ b and can notice the swap symmetry, but the dyad itself has not yet been given a rule of succession.

Accordingly, this chapter introduces no resolving condition F. Asking resolution to do that work here would blur the joint with Distinction.

Difference = non-equivalence; Distinction = non-equivalence made operative
