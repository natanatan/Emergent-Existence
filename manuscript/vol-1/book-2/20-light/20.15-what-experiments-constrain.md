# 20.15 What Experiments Constrain

Optical experiments do not constrain one property at a time. They reveal an interdependent structure. Frequency and wavelength vary together at a fixed speed. Polarization reveals a transverse orientation. Interference depends on relative phase. Reflection and refraction preserve or redirect propagation at boundaries. Radiation pressure shows forward push and Beth's experiment shows rotational push. Photoelectric measurements show energy delivered in units of hf.

Two further results need the same care for both accounts. Kimble, Dagenais and Mandel (1977) counted how often two detectors behind a beam splitter fired together for light from single atoms and found fewer joint detections than a smoothly divided field would give. Hong, Ou and Mandel (1987) found that two excitations entering a beam splitter from opposite sides leave together far more often than independent arrivals would. Both results are counts; their reading as evidence for particles of light depends on a model of how detectors respond to a field. The standard account meets them with a quantized field, the photon reading. For the first result its prediction came before the measurement (Carmichael and Walls 1976)†, so there the photon reading is predicted; whether a prediction preceded the second result is still to be checked. Our closure proposal presently meets them only in part (20.18, 20.21). The closure reading therefore remains untested against their full statistical structure.

Finally, high-energy collisions show light taking part in interactions that produce states with mass (20.23).

The strength of this proposal depends on whether these independently measured relations follow as coordinated consequences of one geometry, rather than as separate behaviors needing separate explanations.
