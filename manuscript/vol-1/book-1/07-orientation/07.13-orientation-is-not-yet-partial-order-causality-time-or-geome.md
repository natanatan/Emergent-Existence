# 7.13 Orientation Is Not Yet Partial Order, Causality, Time or Geometry

Four collapses must be prevented explicitly.

Orientation is not partial order. A directed dependency need not be transitive or acyclic.

Orientation is not physical causality. Dependency may be logical, structural or dynamical without establishing relativistic causal influence.

Orientation is not metric time. The symbol ≺ₒ and the sequence indices express generative and dependency organization, not duration.

Orientation is not spatial direction. No left or right, angle, coordinate axis, length or embedding has been derived.

Causal set theory is a useful comparison because it deliberately begins with a stronger structure, a locally finite partial order interpreted as proto-causality. That order encodes substantial spacetime information, but even there continuum geometry requires further conditions and reconstruction (Surya, 2019). We stay weaker: it asks how directed dependency itself can be derived before assuming the partial-order axioms causal-set theory begins from.
