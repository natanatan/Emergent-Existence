# 13.8 Physical Consequence Without Presupposing Matter

Calling this organization physical risks circularity unless the term is constrained. At this stage, physical does not mean material.

A narrower criterion is available: a geometric distinction becomes physically consequential when changing it changes the admissible relational organization of other instantiated structures rather than merely changing an investigator’s description.

Let Adm(A,B \| Geo) denote the set of couplings admissible between A and B under geometric organization Geo. If Geo ≠ Geo’ and this difference produces Adm(A,B \| Geo) ≠ Adm(A,B \| Geo’), then the geometric distinction has consequences for relational organization.

▪ physical spatiality = geometry with constitutive relational consequences

▪ physical ≠ material

Matter can arrive later.
