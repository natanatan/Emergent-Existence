# 7.3 What the Minimally Resolved Origin Gives This Chapter

Origin defines Ω as the minimally resolved reference condition: no operative distinction is available within the framework’s initial resolving frame. This is a resolution-relative claim, not an assertion that every possible description of Ω is absolutely featureless. Orientation inherits that restraint; it does not require hidden structure beneath Ω.

▪ Res<sub>W₀</sub>(Ω) = ∅ does not entail that no distinction exists at any deeper resolving level

Here W₀ is the least-resolving frame admitted at Origin. The framework neither asserts nor denies finer unresolved variation. Orientation asks a separate question: when can a non-equivalence already available somewhere in the relational organization become consequential at a higher organizational level?

This framing avoids forcing Orientation to explain how an absolutely structureless state generates asymmetry ex nihilo. The chapter can instead ask which admissible variations become integrated into organizations whose consequences survive higher-order resolution and closure.

▪ The resolution-relative definition of Origin is compatible with derived orientation without positing hidden directional structure. Open question: whether unresolved lower-order variation is needed at all, or whether orientation can arise entirely from already-admitted relational organization (Question 5).
