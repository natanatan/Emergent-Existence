# 7.7 How Much Persistence Is Enough?

“Persists” is underspecified on its own. The criterion here is functional: the asymmetry must persist through enough relational transformation to remain consequential when the recursive organization tests re-satisfaction of F. Suppose the sequence reaches a configuration Σ<sub>k</sub> that again belongs to O<sub>F</sub>:

▪ Σ<sub>k</sub> ∈ O<sub>F</sub>

The higher-order identity criterion is again satisfied even though the resulting configuration need not reproduce the earlier realization. Orientation counts as structurally persistent when the orientation-bearing asymmetry changes either the possibility of that re-satisfaction or the relational condition in which it occurs.

▪ orientation persistence threshold = the minimum retention required to affect anchor-relative identity re-entry

This avoids requiring indefinite persistence. An asymmetry is genuine orientation if it survives exactly as long as it takes to alter a later closure or return condition. One that appears and vanishes without affecting any higher-order dependency is a transient perturbation instead.

It uses only re-satisfaction of the identity criterion F and the representative-independent addressability earned in Closure. Recurrence, departure, exact versus non-identical return, path consequence, winding, and phase remain Return’s subject. The dependence therefore runs from Closure’s identity machinery into Orientation, not from a yet-undefined return predicate backward into this chapter.

▪ Persistence of orientation can be defined functionally by consequence for later identity re-entry rather than by duration. Open question: whether re-entry compatibility is the right threshold or only one available criterion (Question 6).
