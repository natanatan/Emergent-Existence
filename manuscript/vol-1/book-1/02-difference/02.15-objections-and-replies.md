# 2.15 Objections and Replies

**Objection 1:** The chapter simply assumes two things. **Reply:** It assumes primitive dyadicity, and it says so. The claim is not that two fully individuated substances are primitive, but that the least constructive structure currently available contains two co-constituted poles. If a genuinely weaker construction later reproduces that work, Proposition 2A should be reduced.

---

**Objection 2:** The relation ≬ already presupposes its relata. **Reply:** The chapter does not claim metaphysical priority of relation over relata. It introduces the dyadic whole, poles, and relation together. Co-constitution avoids both an object-first and a relation-first sequence.

---

**Objection 3:** Weak discernibility is too weak. **Reply:** Agreed if it is asked to generate numerical multiplicity. It is used only to characterize how little qualitative individuation is required once dyadicity has been admitted.

---

**Objection 4:** Exchange symmetry already gives an operation. **Reply:** No. An automorphism visible in a representation is not yet an operation admitted by the modeled structure. Treating those as equivalent would collapse Chapter 3 into vocabulary.

---

**Objection 5:** Why should Difference exist at all? **Reply:** This chapter does not answer that ultimate question. It identifies the minimum constructive commitment from which the remainder of the program will attempt to proceed.

---

**Objection 6:** If Origin has no resolvable distinction, how can Difference follow it? **Reply:** The order is derivational, not temporal. Origin states the least-resolved starting condition; Difference is the first additional structure the framework admits. No physical production event is implied.
