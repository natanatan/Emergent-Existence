# 3.7 Why ≬ and 𝒰 Are Not the Same Thing

A referee can immediately notice that, extensionally, ≬ and 𝒰<sub>min</sub> may contain the same ordered pairs. In the minimal two-pole case both can be represented by {(a,b),(b,a)}. If the chapter relies on extension alone, it has renamed Difference rather than derived Distinction.

The distinction is instead one of type and inferential role:

▪ ≬ constrains what differs; 𝒰 constrains what may follow

From ≬ alone, nothing follows about successors, composition, branching, determinism, or histories. From 𝒰, those questions become well-typed. Two relations can therefore share an extension while making different assertions because they occupy different formal roles in the structure.

This claim is load-bearing. If no downstream formal consequence distinguishes the role of 𝒰 from the role of ≬, Chapter 3 collapses back into Chapter 2.
