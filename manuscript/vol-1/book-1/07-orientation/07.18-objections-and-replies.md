# 7.18 Objections and Replies

**Objection 1:** The chapter renames asymmetry as orientation. **Reply:** Not every asymmetry qualifies. Orientation additionally requires entry into relational dependency and enough persistence to alter higher-order return. Latent and transient asymmetries are excluded by construction (7.4, 7.7).

---

**Objection 2:** K<sub>ij</sub> ≁ K<sub>ji</sub> already assumes direction. **Reply:** K<sub>ij</sub> is introduced as a bookkeeping relation for how admissible variation in one component changes another. The test is role exchange: if swapping i and j leaves all F-relevant possibilities invariant, there is no orientation. The arrow is assigned only after non-equivalence is established.

---

**Objection 3:** Making Ω only resolution-symmetric abandons the minimal origin. **Reply:** That definition is already the book’s, adopted in 1.5 for reasons of its own. It makes the origin weaker, not richer: it asserts no hidden furniture and only refuses to infer absolute absence from non-resolution. This chapter inherits it rather than proposing it.

---

**Objection 4:** Unresolved variation is unfalsifiable. **Reply:** Specific hidden variation would be unfalsifiable if freely invented, and the framework does not posit any. What is testable is the derivation: candidate simulations must show higher-order orientation arising without coarse-grained directional bias. If that cannot be done, the resolution-relative origin has not solved the dynamical problem.

---

**Objection 5:** Symmetric rules cannot produce asymmetric outcomes. **Reply:** A symmetric law can admit lower-symmetry solution branches, which is the standard distinction between the symmetry of equations and the symmetry of states. The exact mechanism depends on instability, degeneracy, fluctuation, boundary conditions or non-unique dynamics. This chapter uses that as an analogy and leaves the project-specific mechanism open.

---

**Objection 6:** Re-entry-based persistence is circular because Return comes next. **Reply:** Section 7.7 uses only re-satisfaction of the already-earned identity criterion F and representative-independent addressability from Closure. It assumes nothing about departure, recurrence, exactness, path consequence, winding, or phase, all of which remain Return’s subject.

---

**Objection 7:** Anchor-relative irreversibility is secretly time. **Reply:** No duration, metric, past, future or entropy is invoked. The claim is only that applying a reversal transformation to a dependency structure changes whether a recursive configuration re-enters an identity class. That is structural reversibility, not temporal reversal.

---

**Objection 8:** Orientation requires a single axis. **Reply:** The general object is the asymmetric constraint structure, not a line. Several independent orientational modes may coexist (7.11), and a single arrow is the smallest example.

---

**Objection 9:** Differential persistence smuggles in probability or fitness. **Reply:** “Differential” means only that under the declared update and perturbation class, one organization retains F while another does not. No probability measure or optimization principle is required; survival frequencies may be external simulation diagnostics.

---

**Objection 10:** Why should return compatibility select what exists? **Reply:** At this stage it should not be elevated into a universal law. It is a candidate mechanism internal to closed recursive identities, and its value is that it yields a testable distinction between transient asymmetry and orientation that participates in stable higher-order organization. How general it is, computation must decide.
