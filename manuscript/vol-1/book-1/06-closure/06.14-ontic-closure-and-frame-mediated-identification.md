# 6.14 Ontic Closure and Frame-Mediated Identification

A further distinction is required because any closure we describe is accessed through some resolving or analytic procedure. That epistemic fact must not be mistaken for the ontological claim that a conscious observer creates the closure.

Let F<sub>dyn</sub> denote the organization actually preserved by the relational succession, insofar as such an invariant can be derived. Let W be an external resolving frame in Boundary’s sense. The frame has access only to the distinctions its criterion preserves, from which an investigator may construct or infer a candidate identity criterion F<sub>obs</sub>. In general:

▪ F<sub>obs</sub> need not equal F<sub>dyn</sub>

A resolving procedure or investigator can miss a real closure, merge distinct closures, or treat an accidental regularity as identity-relevant. We therefore need a way to constrain F<sub>dyn</sub> independently of retrospective description.

A candidate invariant should earn ontic status only if it does explanatory work under intervention or continuation. Perturbing relations constitutive of the candidate changes whether the organization persists. At the same time, changes outside that constitutive set do not have the same consequence. In computational work this can be tested by preregistered ablations and perturbations rather than by choosing F after our result is known.

▪ ontic closure ≠ frame-mediated identification of closure

This distinction lets Volume I remain pre-conscious. Objects and closed structures need not wait for a mind to recognize them. What is frame-mediated is our access to, and encoding of, their closure; W does not become constitutive of F merely because an investigator uses it to detect F.
