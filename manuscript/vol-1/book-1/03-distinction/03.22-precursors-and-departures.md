# 3.22 Precursors and Departures

**Precursors.** Relations on configuration sets, successor relations, composition, identity maps, automorphisms and covariance are standard mathematical machinery. Recursive and rewriting approaches, including Isaacson and Kauffman (2016) and the broader Wolfram-model family (Wolfram, 2020), show that simple update rules can generate rich higher-order structure.

**What is new here.** The chapter separates primitive non-equivalence from admissible transformation by type and role: one states static non-equivalence, the other constrains succession. It makes admissible transformation an explicit assumption cost, forbids defining admissibility through prior resolution, and requires covariance under the dyad’s automorphisms, so that reciprocal exchange follows from symmetry rather than from labels. The claim under test is that this yields an internally operative Distinction while withholding time, orientation, retention and observer-relative resolution.
