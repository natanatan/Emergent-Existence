# 4.5 Frame-Relative Resolvability Returns Here

Chapter 3 deliberately removed a generic resolver from the constitution of Distinction. Boundary is the first place a resolving frame is again needed, because retention is now a question about whether a distinction remains accessible after transformation. The frame does not create the underlying Difference or make succession internal; it evaluates recoverability in a transformed structure.

Let W denote a resolving frame: a structural criterion or map that determines which distinctions are accessible at a declared organizational level. W need not be a conscious observer, sensor, coordinate chart, spatial frame, or reference pole. It is a criterion of discriminatory access.

Keep Origin’s Res notation set-valued. For any configuration Σ,

▪ Res<sub>W</sub>(Σ) = the set of distinctions resolvable from Σ under W

Origin’s Res<sub>W₀</sub>(Ω)=∅ is therefore a special case of the same type: at the least-resolving initial frame, no operative distinction is available. Boundary does not redefine Res as a number, truth value, or operation set.

Frame equivalence is then written ~<sub>W</sub>. Two configurations are frame-equivalent when W cannot resolve them apart. This relation is epistemic/structural in the minimal sense of accessibility; it does not imply subjective observation.
