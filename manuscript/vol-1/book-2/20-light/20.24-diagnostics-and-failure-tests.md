# 20.24 Diagnostics and Failure Tests

The proposal becomes useful only where it can fail. A successful model of light should:

-   generate stable open closures from explicit update and hand-off rules;

-   keep the electromagnetic orthogonality inherited from Chapter 19 and recover two independent transverse polarization degrees of freedom in the free-propagation regime (20.3);

-   distinguish the local update scale from the wavelength, and the wavelength from the envelope;

-   produce interference from signed addition, including exact zeros, rather than inserting wave superposition as a primitive;

-   resolve energy in units of hf at interaction while propagation stays continuous;

-   produce reflection and refraction as outcomes of interaction geometry;

-   deliver forward push E/c and rotational push ±ħ per absorbed unit at every frequency.

The proposal should be revised if:

-   explicit rules produce durable open closures only under arbitrary fine-tuning;

-   wavelength, orthogonality or the propagation limit have to be inserted directly into the rules;

-   the hand-off rule makes speed depend on frequency at any measurable level (20.19);

-   the medium acquires a rest frame detectable by local measurements of light's speed;

-   interference predictions disagree with measured fringes;

-   energy fails to balance across emission, absorption or scattering;

-   the opposed internal charge states of 20.17 leave a net charge on light at a measurable level;

-   $L_{\text{closure}}$ and the transverse handedness χ prove mathematically redundant, or $L_{\text{closure}}$ has no measurable consequence at all (20.18).

The strongest test remains constructive: begin from the relational structures already earned, implement minimal local electromagnetic update and hand-off rules, and see whether stable propagating organizations with the observed families of luminous behavior emerge without being encoded as targets.
