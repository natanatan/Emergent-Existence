# 9.9 Equivalence Is Not Yet Congruence

Boundary through Return rely on identity equivalence, written Σ<sub>a</sub> ~<sub>F</sub> Σ<sub>b</sub>. That two configurations instantiate the same higher-order identity does not yet guarantee that admissible succession treats their equivalence classes compatibly.

Universal algebra names an equivalence relation compatible with the relevant operations a congruence. If equivalent inputs always give equivalent outputs, the classes inherit a well-defined quotient structure. This is the formal precedent closest to the addressability criterion of 6.12. For a deterministic special case compatibility requires:

▪ Σ<sub>a</sub> ~<sub>F</sub> Σ<sub>b</sub> ⇒ QF(𝒰\[Σ<sub>a</sub>\]) = QF(𝒰\[Σ<sub>b</sub>\])

For a branching 𝒰 the criterion is weaker and trajectory-sensitive. Equivalent representatives must have successor behavior that can be matched at the quotient level.

▪ equivalence ⇝ transformation-compatible equivalence ⇝ quotient-level dynamics

We should not declare every F-equivalence a congruence. It should ask which identity criteria are strong enough to support one.

▪ Equivalence and compatibility are different demands, and only the second supports quotient dynamics. Open question: whether exact congruence is too strong for physical and biological systems (Question 10).
