# 13.14 Space Need Not Be Fundamentally Smooth

Once a spatial domain is recovered at one scale, it is tempting to infer that its smoothness belongs to the substrate. That inference is not warranted.

Let ℓ<sub>r</sub> denote a characteristic scale of unresolved relational organization and ℓ<sub>F</sub> the effective resolving scale of a probing frame. When ℓ<sub>r</sub> ≪ ℓ<sub>F</sub>, many lower-order distinctions may be integrated into one resolved neighborhood.

▪ Geo<sub>F</sub> ~ Geo<sub>smooth</sub>

The correct claim is therefore that effective smoothness does not imply fundamental continuity.

Causal-set theory provides a mature example of the broader possibility that discrete underlying organization can approximate continuum spacetime. Its specific causal partial order is not being adopted here (Bombelli, Lee, Meyer, & Sorkin, 1987).

Loop-quantum-gravity constructions likewise provide precedent for treating quantities associated with geometry, such as area and volume, as possessing discrete spectra in a deeper description rather than presupposing a fundamentally continuous geometry. Spin networks and the loop representation show that smooth effective geometry and fine-scale discreteness are not logically incompatible (Rovelli and Smolin, 1995a, 1995b).
