# 8.9 Closure and Return Must Remain Distinct

Closure concerns the sufficiency and organization of constitutive dependencies. Return concerns re-entry into an identity class after departure. Neither entails the other.

▪ Closed<sub>F</sub>(ℛ) ⇏ Ret(A<sub>F</sub>; ·)

A closed organization may transform into a different closed organization and never recover its former identity. Conversely, a recurrent pattern may be imposed by a larger system without possessing the internal dependency sufficiency Closure requires.

Theoretical biology later combines related ideas in a much richer setting. Closure-of-constraints accounts characterize biological organization through mutually dependent constraints that maintain one another, explicitly within thermodynamic and time-scale assumptions unavailable here. Their relevance is prospective: a living system may eventually require both organizational closure and robust recurrent restoration. However, neither concept should be loaded with biological meaning at this stage (Montévil and Mossio, 2015).

▪ closure + robust return ≠ life, but may become one prerequisite for self-maintenance
