# 9.1 Where Return Leaves Us

Chapters 2 through 8 have given us local tools: Difference, admissible succession, retained distinction, mutual constraint, closure, endogenous reference, orientation and Return. Formalism changes perspective from using those structures locally to asking whether they fit inside one coherent formal frame without changing their ontological types.

From that vantage we can compare configurations, follow admissible transitions, tell frame equivalence from identity equivalence, and track retained and oriented dependency. We can also ask when a higher-order identity O<sub>F</sub> supports representative-independent dynamics, with its organizational anchor A<sub>F</sub> remaining a role of that identity rather than a primitive point.

The danger grows with the power of the view. A convenient graph, matrix, quotient, or transition system can clarify our ontology. It can also smuggle in structure our ontology never earned. Our task is to keep encoding and commitment separate.

The task is twofold. We need a formal carrier in which the earned concepts can coexist and compose. A rule for distinguishing structure that belongs to our ontology from structure introduced only because a particular encoding makes calculation easy. The foundational question is what the weakest common formal structure is in which identity, transformation, closure, orientation and return can be represented together. At the same time, every stronger mathematical commitment remains either derived or explicitly external.

▪ Seven chapters of local mathematical devices can be stated in one place, which is a precondition for checking them against each other. Open question: whether the carrier proposed here is the weakest adequate one (Question 1).
