# 9.4 The Minimum Relational Signature

A candidate carrier can be stated very weakly. Let 𝒞 be a collection of admissible relational configurations, ℜ the family of relations resolved within those configurations, and 𝒰 the admissible transition relation between configurations.

▪ 𝔊 = (𝒞, ℜ, 𝒰)

𝒫, ≺, ≺ₒ, ~<sub>W</sub>, ~<sub>F</sub>, O<sub>F</sub>, A<sub>F</sub> and Ret(A<sub>F</sub>;·) are not added as primitive components of 𝔊: they are inherited or derived structures defined over the carrier.

𝒞 is not assumed to be a vector space, manifold, topological space, probability space or metric state space. It is only the collection over which the earned relational descriptions range. ℜ is not restricted in advance to binary relations. 𝒰 retains the type fixed in Distinction: an admissible transition relation on configurations. Formalism consolidates that typing rather than revising it.

▪ 𝒰 ⊆ 𝒞 × 𝒞, where Σ 𝒰 Σ′ means that Σ′ is an admissible successor configuration of Σ

A binary relation can be represented as a subset of a Cartesian product. Relational composition can be defined without assuming uniqueness of output. Standard relation theory supplies the mature analogue. Closure, orientation and return are not added as new primitive components of 𝔊. They remain predicates or derived structures over it.
