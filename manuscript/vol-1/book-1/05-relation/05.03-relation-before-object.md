# 5.3 Relation Before Object

An object-first description begins with things and asks how they relate. This framework attempts the reverse. It asks whether a state-position can become identifiable because of the organization of relations it participates in, before any rich intrinsic object has been assumed.

Define the relational profile of q<sub>i</sub> as the ordered collection of the relations it participates in:

▪ λ<sub>i</sub> = (r<sub>i1</sub>, r<sub>i2</sub>, …, r<sub>in</sub>)

If two state-positions have different relational profiles, λ<sub>i</sub> ≠ λ<sub>j</sub>, they are structurally distinguishable even though we have assigned neither an intrinsic property. our proposal is not that every object is nothing but a row of a matrix. It is that individuation need not begin with intrinsic substance. A stable relational position can do some of the work ordinarily assigned to intrinsic identity.

This is a higher-order recurrence of the weak-discernibility problem isolated in Difference. At the primitive level, numerical dyadicity is admitted explicitly while symmetric irreflexive non-equivalence supplies relational discernibility.

At the present level, a relational position becomes distinguishable by the pattern of retained relations connecting it to the rest of an organized domain. Ontic structural realism makes a related move for physical objects (Ladyman and Ross, 2007; French, 2014). The difference here is that the framework is attempting to earn object-like identity from a thinner inherited scaffold rather than assume it about mature physics.

An object’s primitive identity may be its stable position within a relational organization rather than an intrinsic property carried independently of that organization.
