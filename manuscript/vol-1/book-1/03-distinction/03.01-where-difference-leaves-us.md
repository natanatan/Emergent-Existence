# 3.1 Where Difference Leaves Us

Chapter 2 leaves the primitive dyad 𝔇 = ⟨a,b;≬⟩. Its poles are non-equivalent but exchange-symmetric. The theory can describe the swap a ↔ b, yet nothing in Difference says that one pole may succeed the other. The structure is static in precisely that limited sense.

Foundational question. What is the weakest non-circular additional structure under which primitive non-equivalence becomes internally operative rather than merely analyst-visible?
