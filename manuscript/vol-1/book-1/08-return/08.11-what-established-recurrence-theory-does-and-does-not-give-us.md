# 8.11 What Established Recurrence Theory Does and Does Not Give Us

▬ ESTABLISHED

Classical dynamical systems contain rigorous recurrence theorems. Under measure-preserving dynamics on a finite-measure accessible state space, Poincaré recurrence implies that almost every point of a region returns arbitrarily close to its initial condition under suitable assumptions. Return maps likewise study repeated intersections of trajectories with selected sections (Saussol, 2009).

These results are valuable analogues because they show that mathematically serious recurrence need not mean literal duplication of a complete microstate. But they are not derivations of this chapter. They presuppose state spaces, measures, dynamical evolution and evolution parameters richer than the present ontology. Their role here is diagnostic: later Formalism may legitimately recover or approximate such tools once their prerequisites have been earned.

▪ Return is pre-metric; Poincaré recurrence is a later mathematical analogue
