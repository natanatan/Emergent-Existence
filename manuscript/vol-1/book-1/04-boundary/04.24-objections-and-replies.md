# 4.24 Objections and Replies

**Objection 1:** 𝔗<sub>test</sub> is a third primitive disguised as methodology. **Reply:** Not at this stage. It is the metalanguage domain over which robustness is quantified, analogous to perturbation or quotient analysis. A third ontological commitment appears only if later physics requires an irreducibly primitive family of actually realized transformations.

---

**Objection 2:** Chapter 3 rejected analyst-visible structure, while Chapter 4 relies on analyst-declared transformations. **Reply:** The standards differ because the claims differ. Chapter 3 is constitutive: succession must be internal for Difference to become operative. Chapter 4 is counterfactual: retention is a modal robustness property of an already-operative distinction. A test transformation need not itself be a constituent of the tested system.

---

**Objection 3:** Constant maps reintroduce stasis. **Reply:** No. A constant T<sub>c</sub> is a diagnostic transformation of the representation/structure, not an internal successor pair in 𝒰<sub>min</sub>. The absence of primitive self-transitions remains intact.

---

**Objection 4:** Frame-relative retention makes information observer-dependent. **Reply:** W is a structural resolving criterion, not necessarily an observer. The framework exposes resolution dependence without identifying it with subjectivity. Later physical frames must be earned as realized structures.

---

**Objection 5:** r<sub>ab</sub> is just ≬ with a new name. **Reply:** r<sub>ab</sub> carries an additional modal and historical fact: operative non-equivalence has remained recoverable across the declared variation regime. Bare ≬ states only the static relation in the primitive dyad.

---

**Objection 6:** ≺ smuggles Orientation or Time into Boundary. **Reply:** ≺ records retained dependency between configurations: removing an inherited consequence changes admissibility. It does not orient the internal roles of a relation, measure duration, or imply a temporal partial order. Orientation and Time must add further constraints.

---

**Objection 7:** ~<sub>F</sub> requires an anchor. **Reply:** An identity criterion can classify configurations without privileging a center. An anchor may later be useful or emergent, but it is not logically constitutive of criterion-based equivalence.

---

**Objection 8:** Information has been defined too weakly. **Reply:** Deliberately. Retained consequence is proposed as a minimum functional precursor. Quantitative information theories remain later specializations with stronger assumptions.
