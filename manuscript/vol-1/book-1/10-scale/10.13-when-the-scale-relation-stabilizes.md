# 10.13 When the Scale Relation Stabilizes

Write the successive comparative ratios as mag<sub>n+1</sub>/mag<sub>n</sub>. If they approach a stable value λ\*,

▪ mag<sub>n+1</sub>/mag<sub>n</sub> → λ\*

then λ\* functions as a stable scale factor for that recursive regime. Not every recursion converges: a ratio can oscillate, cycle, stay bounded without settling, or behave irregularly. Scale is not defined here as convergence. What the chapter establishes is that recursive composition can produce a stable comparative relation, and that when it does, the stability belongs to the relation rather than to any level’s magnitude.

One more confusion is worth closing:

▪ recursion depth ≠ scale

The index n records which stage of the derivation we are at. It says nothing about how large anything is.
