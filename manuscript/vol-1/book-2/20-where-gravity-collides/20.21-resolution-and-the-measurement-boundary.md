# 20.21 Resolution and the Measurement Boundary

Book I defined resolution relative to a frame: a distinction unresolved in one frame may be resolvable in another. This chapter gives that structure a physical form. A distinction becomes resolvable only through a coupling that can produce differing records, so resolution is not just a choice of description; in an experiment it is a physical interaction.

Two frames must be kept apart. W_0 is the initial resolving frame from which the derivation began, the frame of Origin. W_obs is the finest frame that observation currently reaches. Where a frame resolves nothing further about a structure X, Book I writes Res_W(X) = ∅: the frame is silent about what lies below it. That silence is not evidence that nothing lies below. Whether W_0 and W_obs are the same frame is an open question, and failing to resolve deeper structure at W_obs does not settle it.

This also sharpens the account of measurement. A finer measurement can expose distinctions that a coarser frame legitimately ignores, but only by coupling to them. If the coupling changes the field, the finer measurement cannot be read as passively revealing a value that existed unchanged beforehand. A distinction obtained through one kind of coupling should not be promoted to a property of the substrate unless it persists across very different couplings.
