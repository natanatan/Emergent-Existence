# 9.12 Structural, Frame and Behavioral Equivalence

The framework now contains several kinds of sameness that must remain notationally distinct. Identity equivalence, Σ<sub>a</sub> ~<sub>F</sub> Σ<sub>b</sub>, says that the configurations satisfy the same identity criterion F. Frame equivalence, Σ<sub>a</sub> ~<sub>W</sub> Σ<sub>b</sub>, says that resolving frame W cannot discriminate them. Behavioral equivalence, written provisionally Σ<sub>a</sub> ≈𝒰 Σ<sub>b</sub>, says that they support matching patterns of admissible succession.

▪ ~<sub>F</sub>, ~<sub>W</sub> and ≈<sub>𝒰</sub> are not interchangeable

These can overlap without being identical. Two configurations may be structurally the same object while a finer frame resolves internal differences. They may be frame-equivalent yet behave differently under a transformation invisible to that frame. And behavioral equivalence can be stricter than the identity criterion F. The distinction stops the book sliding between ontic identity, observer resolution and dynamical similarity.
