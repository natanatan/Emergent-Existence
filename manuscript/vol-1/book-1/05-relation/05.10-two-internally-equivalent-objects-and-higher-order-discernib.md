# 5.10 Two Internally Equivalent Objects and Higher-Order Discernibility

Object-like persistence produces a new version of the problem that began the book. Suppose two persistent organizations OA and OB satisfy the same internal identity criterion. Can a larger resolving frame still preserve them as two distinct members of one domain rather than collapse them into one effective class?

The question must be stated carefully. Numerical distinctness and frame-resolved discernibility are not the same claim. It is logically possible to posit two numerically distinct but perfectly exchangeable organizations. What must be explained is how a larger frame W preserves their non-equivalence as two resolvable members of one domain. If every relation available under W is invariant under exchanging OA and OB, then W contains no criterion that tells their relational positions apart. To preserve two distinguishable positions at the higher level, the larger domain must retain some difference in their relational profiles.

Let λW(O) be the relational profile of organization O as resolved within the larger frame W. Higher-order discernibility requires at least:

▪ λW(OA) ≠ λW(OB)

This does not require the organizations to possess different intrinsic properties. It requires the larger domain to retain a difference in how they participate relationally. The same architecture is thereby reused at a higher organizational level: distinction, retention, identity, then higher-order distinction. The nearby technical literature includes debates over permutation symmetry and the identity of indiscernibles for quanta (French and Redhead, 1988), which ask related questions about entities sharing intrinsic properties.

▪ If two internally equivalent persistent objects coexist observably, some relation in the larger frame must preserve their non-equivalence. Open question: what minimum reference structure suffices to break exchange symmetry (Question 6).
