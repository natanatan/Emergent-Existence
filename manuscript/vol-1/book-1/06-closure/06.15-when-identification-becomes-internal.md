# 6.15 When Identification Becomes Internal

There is nevertheless a later transition in which the distinction between ontic closure and identified closure becomes internal to a system. A system may first preserve its organization without representing that organization. Later, a sufficiently complex system may contain a resolving process that maps aspects of its own state into an internal model:

▪ W<sub>S</sub>(ℛ<sub>S</sub>) ↦ σ<sub>S</sub>

where σ<sub>S</sub> is a self-model, the symbol the notation table reserves for it. If σ<sub>S</sub> represents conditions relevant to the persistence of the same system, closure has become internally represented rather than merely externally identified. That is a qualitatively new level, but it should not be equated immediately with self-consciousness.

▪ self-maintenance ≠ self-discrimination ≠ self-model ≠ recursive self-consciousness

Volume II will ask what additional organization is required for retained distinctions to become distinctions for a system, and later, when a system can represent itself as the bearer of those encodings. Closure needs only the negative boundary condition: no self-model is required for ontic closure.

(forward pointer to Volume II) Closure can exist before it is represented. Its identification is initially external to the closed domain; reflexive cognition begins only when the domain can internally represent and evaluate conditions of its own persistence. Whether that is sufficient for phenomenal self-consciousness remains open.
