# 3.17 Objections and Replies

**Objection 1:** 𝒰 is simply another primitive. **Reply:** Yes, unless it is later derived. The chapter counts it openly because a static Difference cannot by itself license succession. “Fewest possible assumptions” requires minimizing commitments, not pretending that an indispensable commitment was derived when it was not.

---

**Objection 2:** 𝒰<sub>min</sub> and ≬ have the same extension. **Reply:** At the minimal dyad they can. Their type and inferential roles differ: one states non-equivalence; the other licenses successor structure. The chapter fails if that difference never matters downstream.

---

**Objection 3:** Ordered pairs already introduce orientation. **Reply:** An ordered pair represents a succession relation locally, but the full update relation is invariant under exchanging the poles. Orientation requires a persistent asymmetry between exchangeable roles, which is absent.

---

**Objection 4:** Why is (a,b) admissible in the first place? **Reply:** Nontrivial admissibility is primitive here. Symmetry determines its reciprocal partner once one nontrivial transition is admitted, but the existence of transformation itself remains an explicit unexplained commitment.

---

**Objection 5:** A two-state oscillator is already a clock. **Reply:** It exhibits formal recurrence, and an analyst can count compositions, but Time requires four criteria for clockhood: recurrence, countability, stability, and couplability. Here recurrence is formal; the composition count is still analyst bookkeeping rather than an earned physical measure; stability under perturbation has not yet been shown; and no coupling to an independent recurrent process exists. J<sub>𝒰</sub>² = id is therefore recurrence without yet being a physical clock.

---

**Objection 6:** Excluding self-loops is arbitrary. **Reply:** The exclusion follows the minimality rule. Primitive self-loops add stasis and, when combined with cross-transitions, branching. Neither is required to make Difference operative. They may be admitted later if a downstream construction needs them.

---

**Objection 7:** J<sub>𝒰</sub> is still an analyst’s function. **Reply:** Correct as notation. What grounds it is not the analyst’s ability to write a swap but the antecedent internal transition relation 𝒰. J<sub>𝒰</sub> is only the functional representation of a relation already admitted in the model.
