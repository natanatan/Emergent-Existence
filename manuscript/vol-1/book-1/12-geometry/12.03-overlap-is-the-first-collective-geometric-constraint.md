# 12.3 Overlap Is the First Collective Geometric Constraint

Neighborhoods become geometrically informative when they overlap in constrained ways. If B belongs to a neighborhood of A and C belongs to a neighborhood of B, the organization may limit which relations between A and C remain admissible. The total pattern of overlaps then carries information that no isolated distance contains.

Let D denote the family of admissible relational distances. A geometry requires that members of D are not freely assignable independently of one another. The exact mathematical form will depend on the metric structure eventually recovered, but the conceptual condition is simple: changing one local relation can constrain several others.

This gives geometry a recognizable ontological level. Distance measured separation between loci. Geometry concerns organized constraints among systems of separations.

distance : relation between loci

geometry : relation among systems of distances

The distinction prevents the chapter from simply renaming a metric space as emergent geometry. A metric supplies the raw comparative structure. Geometry appears only where that structure acquires stable higher-order organization.
