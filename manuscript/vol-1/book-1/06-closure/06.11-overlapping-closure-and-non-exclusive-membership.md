# 6.11 Overlapping Closure and Non-Exclusive Membership

Closure does not require reality to partition into mutually exclusive containers. A retained relation can participate in more than one persistent organization if it contributes to distinct identity-relevant invariants.

▪ r<sub>i</sub> ∈ ℛ<sub>A</sub> and r<sub>i</sub> ∈ ℛ<sub>B</sub>

provided the two closures are defined by different but compatible relational organizations F<sub>A</sub> and F<sub>B</sub>. Nothing in the pre-geometric ontology requires ℛ<sub>A</sub> ∩ ℛ<sub>B</sub> = ∅.

This matters because recursive emergence is more likely to be heterarchical than a perfectly nested tree. Later biological and social systems plainly exhibit overlapping organization. The present result is structural rather than empirical: the closure definition itself contains no exclusivity axiom.

Overlap raises a real pressure point. If two closures demand incompatible states of the same relation, the relation cannot satisfy both simultaneously without additional machinery. Such conflict may require retained-dependency precedence, alternation, contextual resolution or a later reconciliation formalism. Closure should not solve that problem by assuming a global arbitration rule.

▪ Closure need not imply exclusive membership. Open question: what formal conditions permit compatible overlap, and what happens when two candidate closures impose contradictory constraints on a shared relation.
