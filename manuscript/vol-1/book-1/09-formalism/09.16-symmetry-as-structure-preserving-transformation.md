# 9.16 Symmetry as Structure-Preserving Transformation

Orientation used exchange tests to distinguish symmetric from oriented dependency roles, and formalism can state the idea generally. Let g be a relabeling or transformation of the formal structure. If all relevant relations are preserved, g is an automorphism of that encoding. Such transformations form a group under composition.

▪ g(𝔊) ≅ 𝔊

Orientation appears when an exchange that previously preserved the relevant structure no longer does.

▪ g<sub>swap</sub>(𝔊<sub>oriented</sub>) ≇ 𝔊<sub>oriented</sub>

This gives Orientation a cleaner formal reading. The arrow records a broken interchangeability of roles, not a primitive direction. One caution matters. Orientation should not be defined as a smaller automorphism group, since different structural changes reduce symmetry for unrelated reasons. The narrower statement is that the exchange transformation of the roles in question ceases to preserve the constraint organization.
