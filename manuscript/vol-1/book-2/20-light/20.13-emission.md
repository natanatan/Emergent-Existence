# 20.13 Emission

If light is a coherent open closure propagating across space, how does one begin? More specifically, how can energy retained within a localized persistent organization become reorganized into a self-maintaining propagating relation?

Emission is a transition between two modes of energetic persistence. Before emission, energy is retained within a localized organization. After emission, part of that energy is maintained through continuous re-instantiation across neighboring relations.

Chapter 21 earns what a localized persistent organization is, and an atom is the most familiar example. For now it is enough that such an organization can hold energy in distinct configurations, and that when it changes from one configuration to a lower one, the energy difference must go into some other admissible organization. One continuation is an open closure:

$$\text{localized state} \rightarrow \text{transition} \rightarrow \text{released energy} \rightarrow \text{open closure}$$

Other continuations are possible:

-   coherent open closure, which is radiative emission;

-   incoherent redistribution into many local modes, which is non-radiative transfer (heat);

-   a transition organized from outside, which is stimulated emission or a collision.

The available continuations determine which outcome occurs. This also explains why emission has directional structure. The source's geometry does not select one predetermined ray, but it constrains which outgoing orientations are compatible, so the geometry of the emitting organization shapes the geometry of the emitted light.
