# 10.17 Scale as a Relational Fixed Point

The case generalizes. Let R be a map on comparative ratios, taken in the analyst’s metalanguage rather than as a relation of the modelled world. As a result, that r<sub>n+1</sub> = R(r<sub>n</sub>). A stable scale factor is then a fixed point:

▪ R(λ*) = λ*

The underlying organization may keep changing while its comparative relation holds steady. This echoes a lesson the book has now used three times: higher-order invariance survives lower-order transformation. Scale is therefore compatible with a dynamic ontology. Stability of relation does not require stasis of state.

One caution about the type. R is written as a function because ratios are numbers and the analyst may compute with them. The modelled transition 𝒰 remains a relation (9.5). Where branching makes several successor magnitudes admissible, a stable scale factor need not be unique.
