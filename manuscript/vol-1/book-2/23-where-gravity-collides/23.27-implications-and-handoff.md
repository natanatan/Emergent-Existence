# 23.27 Implications and Handoff

This chapter has proposed where the quantum world begins and ends. It begins where an object's alternatives are finer than its surroundings can resolve, at the scale of its de Broglie wavelength. It ends where those alternatives would condition the relational field into local geometries that cannot coexist, and one is retained. Between those edges, the field's parts combine with a definite relation between their cycles, which is interference; measurement is a physical coupling that can put their differences on record; and entangled systems are organizations directly related in an order deeper than the space they produce. A detection is one interaction running from emitter to detector, retained in the form that is consistent at both ends, so collapse is not a separate process. ◌ The self-conditioning that forces a single outcome at a detector is, on this proposal, the same conditioning that appears at large scale as gravity.

This earns a research program with definite next steps: define the field variables needed for phase and interference; write an explicit coupling and feedback rule; define the incompatibility measure and test where its threshold falls against existing bounds; check conservation and timescale; and run repeated simulations to see whether the Born frequencies and Bell correlations emerge without being written in.

One distinction should be kept clear. The "localized, retained organizations" of Chapters 22 and 23 are the general organizations earned in Book I's Closure. Matter is the physical case of them, proposed in Chapter 21 (◌): a localized excitation becomes matter when it changes from an oriented projection across space into a localized, persistent self-transformation. By feeding its own transformation it develops the inertia that increases its stability and persistence. As it winds more tightly around itself, its angular momentum increases, and with it its frequency. A collection of such excitations that coalesce into a stable formation is impenetrable to other forms of matter that do not share its exact composition topology.
