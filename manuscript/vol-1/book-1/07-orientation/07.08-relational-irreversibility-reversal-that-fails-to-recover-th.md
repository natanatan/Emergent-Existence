# 7.8 Relational Irreversibility: Reversal That Fails to Recover the Anchor

Orientation is weaker than irreversibility. A dependency structure can be oriented while a reversed organization still recovers the same higher-order identity. The pre-temporal notion of irreversibility proposed here is therefore anchor-relative.

Let 𝔒 be an orientation structure and let Ret(A<sub>F</sub>; 𝔒) indicate whether an admissible sequence organized under 𝔒 can depart from and re-enter O<sub>F</sub> (the predicate is defined explicitly in 8.3). The map 𝔒 ↦ 𝔒⁻¹ denotes role reversal of the orientational structure; it is a transformation in the analyst’s representation, not retained precedence. If:

▪ Ret(A<sub>F</sub>; 𝔒) = 1 while Ret(A<sub>F</sub>; 𝔒⁻¹) = 0

then reversal fails to reconstruct the same identity-re-entry relation, and the organization is relationally irreversible with respect to A<sub>F</sub>.

Working proposition. Relational irreversibility appears when reversing the orientational dependency conditions destroys, changes, or redirects re-entry into the identity class referenced by A<sub>F</sub>.

This is not yet thermodynamic irreversibility and not an arrow of physical time. It is a structural non-equivalence between forward and reversed dependency organizations. Later chapters can ask whether physical temporal irreversibility is a special realization of this more primitive architecture.

◌ SPECULATIVE Anchor-relative irreversibility is a candidate precursor of temporal irreversibility, not an instance of it.
