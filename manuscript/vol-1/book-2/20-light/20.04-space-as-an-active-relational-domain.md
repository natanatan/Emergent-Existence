# 20.4 Space as an Active Relational Domain

Book I (Chapter 13) earned space as coordinated localization: neighborhoods that persist because their relations are continually re-established. Chapter 18 earned the invariant rate at which those relations can be coordinated. Space in this framework is therefore never frozen. A neighborhood persists through time only because its relations are repeatedly updated and reconciled with those of its neighbors.

Measurement is consistent with this picture without forcing it. Two uncharged metal plates held a fraction of a micrometre apart in vacuum are pushed together (the Casimir force, measured from the late 1990s†). One reading treats the force as evidence of activity in the vacuum itself. Another computes the same force from the charges in the plates acting on each other, with no vacuum term (Jaffe 2005†). The measurement does not decide between them. We inherit the activity of space from Book I and Chapter 18 rather than deriving it from either reading of the Casimir force.

We can then distinguish ambient activity from organized luminous activity:

$$\text{ambient relational updating} \rightarrow \text{coherent organization} \rightarrow \text{propagating excitation}$$

A region of space can be continually updated without that updating forming a self-maintaining propagating structure. Light appears where the updating carries an organization that keeps its identity across successive neighborhoods.

This continuity reaches back to the earliest dependency developed in the ontology:

$$\text{excitation} \rightarrow \text{permitted difference} \rightarrow \text{recoverable distinction} \rightarrow \text{retention} \rightarrow \text{recursion} \rightarrow \text{organization}$$

Light is a physical realization of that sequence under electromagnetic conditions.
