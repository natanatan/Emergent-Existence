# 20.27 Precursors and Departures

The study of light repeatedly advanced when apparently separate observations were unified by fewer organizing principles.

Christiaan Huygens developed a wave-based geometrical account of propagation and used it for reflection and refraction, treating each wavefront as generated by local propagation from the last [Huygens 1690]. That is the closest precursor to a neighborhood-by-neighborhood hand-off. Thomas Young supplied the experimental case for interference [Young 1802, 1804]; we retain interference as a central constraint and read it as signed compatibility among overlapping closures.

Michael Faraday tied optical behavior to magnetic organization and developed the conception of lines of force [Faraday 1846]. Wilhelm Weber and Rudolf Kohlrausch found a speed in the ratio of electric to magnetic strengths [Weber and Kohlrausch 1856†]. James Clerk Maxwell formalized electricity and magnetism as one dynamical field and showed that its disturbances travel at the speed of light [Maxwell 1865]. We inherit that unity from Chapter 19 rather than rederiving electromagnetism here. John Henry Poynting identified directed electromagnetic energy flow through the field [Poynting 1884], and later the rotational push carried by circularly polarized light [Poynting 1909†], which Richard Beth measured [Beth 1936†].

Hendrik Lorentz and Henri Poincaré kept a medium and explained why motion through it cannot be detected [Lorentz 1904†]. John Bell argued that this route gives the mechanism special relativity leaves out [Bell 1976†]. Our proposal follows that ontological route, with the difference that the medium is space itself and observers are organizations maintained within its updates.

Max Planck introduced energy exchanged in fixed elements [Planck 1901], and Albert Einstein extended the quantum hypothesis to light itself [Einstein 1905]. The relation E = hf remains a binding empirical constraint. Willis Lamb and Marlan Scully showed that the photoelectric results follow from a continuous field and detectors that absorb in fixed steps [Lamb and Scully 1969†], the precedent for 20.18. John Wheeler and Richard Feynman treated emission and absorption as two ends of one interaction [Wheeler and Feynman 1945†], and Carver Mead built an electrodynamics without photons on that idea [Mead 2000†].

Louis de Broglie (1924) proposed that a particle is accompanied by a wave, helping establish that wave-like organization and particle-like interaction cannot be cleanly assigned to separate kinds of entity [de Broglie 1929]. Modern quantum optics sharpened the problem with counting experiments [Kimble, Dagenais and Mandel 1977; Hong, Ou and Mandel 1987], which constrain our account as they constrain any account of light.

High-energy experiments supply another boundary condition: light scattering off light and producing states with mass [ATLAS Collaboration 2017; 2020].

Across these traditions the measured relations are secure, even where their ontological reading remains open. This proposal keeps the observed structure and asks whether a recursively maintained relational geometry supplies the common mechanism beneath propagation, delivery in units, electromagnetic orientation and interaction-induced transformation.
