# 13.3 Geometric Coherence Is Not Spatial Coordination

Geometry already required coherence. Distances could not simply be arbitrary values. Their higher-order organization had to support stable neighborhoods, compatible transformations, and reproducible structure across scale. But coherence does not yet imply coordination in the present sense.

▪ geometric coherence ≠ spatial coordination

Geometric coherence means that the metric relations fit together as a consistent geometry. Spatial coordination means that geometric localization relations mutually constrain the roles available to instantiated relational structures.

▪ consistent geometric relation ⇝ mutually consequential localization relation

A mathematical geometry can remain unchanged whether or not anything physically depends upon its locations. A spatial geometry cannot.
