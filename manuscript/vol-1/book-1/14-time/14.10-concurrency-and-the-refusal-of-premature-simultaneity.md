# 14.10 Concurrency and the Refusal of Premature Simultaneity

If two quotient events are incomparable under ≺ₜ, the framework should not immediately call them simultaneous. Incomparability means only that the derived temporal candidate does not order them within the relevant frame or domain.

▪ not(\[eₐ\] ≺ₜ \[eᵦ\]) and not(\[eᵦ\] ≺ₜ \[eₐ\]) ⇒ temporally unordered at this stage

Simultaneity is a stronger physical relation. In relativity it is frame-dependent and tied to the structure of spacetime. Dependency-incomparable events may later map to spacelike-separated events, to unresolved ordering, to independent local updates, or to something else entirely. Treating incomparability as simultaneity would prematurely import relativistic interpretation.
