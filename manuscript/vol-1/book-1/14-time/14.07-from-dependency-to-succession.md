# 14.7 From Dependency to Succession

The quotient precedence ≺ₜ becomes a candidate temporal order when three conditions are jointly satisfied. First, it must be reconstructible from retained dependency after external indices are removed. Second, it must remain stable enough under admissible transformations and coarse-graining to support consistent comparisons. Third, distinct resolving frames must recover compatible local orderings wherever they share access to the same dependency structure.

A working definition follows.

Working definition of temporal order. A temporal order is a stable, recursively recoverable strict ordering of relational transformations derived from retained dependency, such that the ordering constrains admissible histories without requiring an external time parameter. Raw ≺ is the inherited dependency relation; ≺ₜ denotes the stronger order earned only after cycle reduction and the additional coherence conditions stated here.

This definition is deliberately weaker than metric time. It tells us what can count as succession. It does not yet tell us how much time separates successive states.
