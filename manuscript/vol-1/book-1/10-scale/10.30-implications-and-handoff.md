# 10.30 Implications and Handoff

Scale began by changing the frame from comparison of kind to comparison of amount. We can now pair organizations, order finite magnitudes, represent proportion, and recognize when a recursive relation stabilizes across levels.

The important change is not that our ontology has acquired a ruler. It has acquired something prior to one: a repeatable relation of magnitude.

▪ correspondence ⇝ equinumerosity ⇝ cardinal order ⇝ cardinal label

That lets us zoom across recursive levels without confusing level with size. A changing organization can preserve a stable proportional relation even while no geometric separation has yet been defined.

▪ relative magnitude ⇝ proportion ⇝ recursive scale

The next shift is decisive. We can now ask how much, but not how far. Distance applies the magnitude we have earned to relational separation and tests whether distance can emerge without being assumed.

None of this is geometry. It is what geometry will need. We can now answer how much, in a limited relational sense. We still cannot answer how far. The difference between those two questions is the whole of the next chapter.

Handoff to Distance. Comparative magnitude exists; landmarks still float without separation. Can the magnitude we have earned be applied to relational separation itself, so that how much becomes how far?

Questions Opened by This Framework

Is cardinal comparison the weakest adequate notion of magnitude, or is something weaker available?

Can the membership and decomposition criteria that fix a magnitude be constrained independently, as F must be?

What distinguishes a recursive regime whose comparative ratio stabilizes from one whose ratio does not?

Does the cross-correspondence of 10.10 extend beyond finite organizations, where products and bijections are unproblematic?

Does anything in the framework select particular recurrence coefficients, or are they always a model choice?

When is a stable ratio a property of the modelled organization rather than of the analyst’s bookkeeping?

What further structure turns a repeatable comparison into a calibrated unit?

Which coarse-grainings preserve the comparative relations that defined them?

Can scale invariance be stated without any metric, and is any relational organization here genuinely self-similar?

How do several scale relations over independent modes interact, if at all?

Does a family of scale relations ever compose into something stronger without importing vector structure?

What would falsify the claim that magnitude here is earned rather than supplied by notation?

Jourdain in *Contributions to the Founding of the Theory of Transfinite Numbers* (Open Court, 1915; reprinted by Dover), where this article begins at page 85. The origin of the modern concept of cardinality, and the source of the one-to-one correspondence this chapter uses without its numerals.

On the Cantor–Schröder–Bernstein theorem, any standard set-theory text. The result is used here only for finite organizations, where it is elementary.

Frege, G. (1884). *Die Grundlagen der Arithmetik: Eine logisch mathematische Untersuchung über den Begriff der Zahl*. Breslau: Wilhelm Koebner. Translated by J. L. Austin as *The Foundations of Arithmetic* (Blackwell, 1950). For sameness of number defined by one-to-one correspondence, the principle Frege took from Hume.

Hume, D. (1739–40). *A Treatise of Human Nature*. 3 vols. London: John Noon (vol. III, Thomas Longman). Book I, Part III, Section 1. The correspondence principle in its original form.
