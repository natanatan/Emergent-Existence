# 12.2 From Pairwise Separation to Metric Neighborhood

For a locus A and an internally available scale ε, consider the family of loci whose relational distance from A lies below that scale. In conventional notation one might write N\_ε(A) = {X : d(A,X) \< ε}. Nothing in this expression requires us to picture a literal ball in a pre-existing space. At this stage it identifies a scale-relative relational neighborhood.

A useful neighborhood must do more than group whatever the analyst chooses to call close. Its membership must follow from the independently constrained distance relation. Neighboring domains should overlap where the metric organization warrants overlap, and those overlaps should remain sufficiently stable under admissible perturbation, relabeling, and coarse-graining.

We can therefore state a first candidate condition: local geometric coherence is the persistence of neighborhood relations under admissible transformation.

▪ local geometric coherence = stable neighborhood relations under admissible transformation

If an arbitrarily small perturbation of an arbitrary descriptive partition completely rewires every neighborhood relation, then the system may possess metric values without possessing a robust geometry. The distinction is the same one that has appeared repeatedly in the series: a structure becomes ontologically interesting when its relevant relations survive transformation rather than depending on the notation used to display them.
