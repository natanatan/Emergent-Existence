# 12.6 Layer Is Not Dimension

The distinction between layer and dimension is non-negotiable. Three layers do not imply three dimensions, and a single layer could in principle support several independent modes of variation.

▪ number of relational layers ≠ number of geometric dimensions

A dimension should enter only when a local state can vary in more than one irreducible way. Suppose a neighborhood around A admits candidate variation modes δ<sub>1</sub>, δ<sub>2</sub>, …, δ<sub>n</sub>. If a change available through one mode cannot be reproduced, under the declared equivalence criterion, by admissible combinations of the others, then that mode contributes an independent degree of variation.

Formalism has already supplied the crucial discipline: independence can be represented before orthogonality. We therefore need not begin with perpendicular axes. An effective dimension is first a rank-like statement about how many independent relational variations are required to preserve the local distinctions relevant at the chosen scale. Geometry therefore reuses Formalism’s d<sub>R</sub> rather than introducing a second rank symbol: the geometric question is whether the already-defined relational rank remains stable and interpretable across the metric-neighborhood regime.

▪ d<sub>R</sub> := rank(Ind<sub>R</sub>), evaluated here on the stable independent relational modes that survive the declared geometric regime

The exact rank structure remains a formal question. Matroidal language is one candidate because it captures independence without requiring an inner product, but the ontology does not depend on that particular mathematical implementation.
