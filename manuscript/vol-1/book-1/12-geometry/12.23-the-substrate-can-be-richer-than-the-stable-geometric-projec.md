# 12.23 The Substrate Can Be Richer Than the Stable Geometric Projection

Consider a substrate that supports many lower-order relational modes δ<sub>1</sub> through δ<sub>N</sub>. Under a particular family of interactions, only a smaller number of collective combinations remain independently resolvable. If three such modes dominate over a broad scale regime, the effective geometry available to those probes may be rank three.

▪ Π<sub>F</sub>(𝔏) ↦ 𝔏<sub>F</sub>^(3) while the underlying 𝔏 may remain structurally richer

Nothing in that statement requires hidden dimensions in the specific sense used by any existing high-energy theory. The unresolved distinctions might instead be strongly coupled, below the probe’s resolution, dynamically suppressed, topologically constrained, or simply irrelevant to the invariants that define spatial position.

The weaker and more defensible proposition is therefore:

▪ observed dimensionality = dimensionality of stable resolvable geometric variation

Whether that dimensionality is also fundamental is a further question.
