# 9.29 Comparison of Candidate Formal Languages

The table ranks candidates for encoding, not for adoption. A language enters our ontology only by the admissibility rule of 9.3. No row below is declared to be our ontology.

| **Formal language** | **What it gives** | **Main risk** | **Role here** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| General relational structure | Arbitrary n-ary relations | Very weak operational guidance | Baseline meta-language |
| Transition system | Admissible state-to-state behavior | May over-emphasize state tokens | Core candidate representation |
| Graph | Pairwise incidence and connectivity | Binary and adjacency bias | Useful representation |
| Hypergraph | Collective n-ary incidence | Higher arity may be representational rather than ontic | Candidate where pairwise encoding distorts |
| Relation algebra | Identity, converse, composition of relations | Binary-relation emphasis | Useful algebraic layer |
| Universal algebra, congruence | Compatibility, quotients, homomorphisms | Operation-first bias | Key tool for higher-order autonomy |
| Coalgebra, bisimulation | Behavior and transition equivalence | High abstraction overhead | Comparison tool |
| Category theory | Typed composition and universal constructions | Too general to constrain ontology by itself | Meta-language, not foundation |
| Simplicial complex | Nested incidence and boundary operators | Topological and geometric over-reading | One later realization |
| Matroid | Independence without vector-space commitment | May not match dynamic dependence | Candidate formalization of d<sub>R</sub> |
| Vector space | Linear combination, rank, basis | Imports scalars, linearity, dimension | Not yet admitted as ontology |
| Topological space | Neighborhood and continuity | Imports topology | Not yet earned |
| Probability space | Weighted alternatives | Imports measure and probability | Not yet earned |

Stronger languages become ontologically relevant only when later derivations license their additional structure.
