# Methodology

This project is not an attempt at a Theory of Everything, and it should not be read as working toward one. It is an attempt to build a minimal, recursively generative ontology and to test how far it can be extended without importing higher-order structure too early. Every inferential bridge is stated, every additional assumption is marked where it enters, and every place the argument currently stops is named as an open problem rather than smoothed over.

The umbrella question this book asks is: how much of observable structure can be reconstructed from minimal logical-relational primitives? Framed this way, the ambition stays intact without implying that the series claims exhaustive unification of physics, mathematics, consciousness and meaning. The working form of that question across the volumes is narrower still and stated as a hypothesis to be tested, not assumed: can increasingly rich physical, biological, cognitive and social structures be derived from a parsimonious, recursively generative relational foundation? The chapters ahead do not assume the answer is yes, and a negative answer, a specific point beyond which no further reduction succeeds, would still leave everything established up to that point intact.

The methodological aspiration follows from this: construct the strongest derivational scaffold possible, identify every unresolved bridge explicitly, and leave those bridges in a form that can be mathematically, computationally or empirically extended by later work. A gap explicitly located is worth more than a conclusion reached by hiding an assumption, and the Hypothesis Status Register in the back exists to make that trade visible rather than implicit. Five standards follow:

1. Minimize primitives.

2. Mark every additional assumption where it enters.

3. Separate formal consequence from physical identification.

4. State plainly where derivation ends and analogy begins.

5. Where a claim cannot yet be derived or tested, name it as a research problem rather than assert it.

Three uses of a concept must be kept apart, because the discipline applies to only one of them.

A **constitutive** use does explanatory work inside a derivation. Here the rule is strict: at a given stage, the ontology may not use a property that stage has not earned. Coordinates cannot explain why geometry exists if coordinates arrive later; metric duration cannot be a premise of a chapter that precedes Time.

A **representational** use describes, in a richer formalism, a structure that has already been derived. This is admissible when it is labelled, and the Formal Admissibility Rule states the condition: extra structure in the notation may not be read back into the world it describes.

A **diagnostic** use applies a later or external framework to the output of a derivation, to ask whether that output reproduces a target property. This too is admissible, and it is how the framework becomes testable at all.

▪ do not use the destination to build the road; once the road is built, use the destination to check whether it arrived

We are not occupying the ontology at its earlier stages. We are present-day investigators with geometry, clocks, fields, computers and mathematics already to hand, and it would be perverse to refuse those instruments when asking whether a pre-geometric proposal actually yields geometry. A pre-geometric model may not assume Euclidean distance; its emergent separations may certainly be compared against Euclidean geometry afterwards. A pre-temporal model may not assume proper time; its intervals may be checked against proper-time relations. A pre-quantum model may not assume Hilbert space; its effective observables may be tested for quantum-statistical behaviour. Forbidding the mature structure as a target would leave the framework almost impossible to falsify.

▪ higher-order structure ∉ premises of the derivation; higher-order structure ∈ diagnostics applied to its outputs

One guard keeps the second use from quietly becoming the first. A diagnostic influences a derivation not by entering its rules but by deciding which model is kept after the result is seen. So the diagnostic, its control and its criterion are declared before the run, and a failed diagnostic is reported rather than retuned away. Where that discipline lapses, the target has been smuggled back in through model selection, which is harder to notice than smuggling it into the premises and does the same damage.

Some claims in what follows are close to definitional. Others are derived within stated assumptions, or demonstrated computationally for a toy model, or conjectural but sharply formulated, or simply markers of where the framework’s reach currently ends. That gradient of confidence is not a weakness in the argument; hiding it would be. If later work replaces parts of this formalism, the scaffold will still have done its job.
