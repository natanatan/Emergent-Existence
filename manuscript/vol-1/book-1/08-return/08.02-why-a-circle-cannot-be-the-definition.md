# 8.2 Why a Circle Cannot Be the Definition

The intuitive picture of return is a circle, but the picture is useful only after its hidden commitments are separated. A drawn cycle already contains directedness, sequence, and a criterion for calling its terminal realization “the same” higher-order identity as its initial realization. In ordinary physics it also suggests elapsed time and a spatial path, neither of which is available here.

We therefore treat the circle as a later encoding, not a primitive definition. The minimally adequate form is ordered departure followed by re-entry into an identity class:

▪ A<sub>F</sub> ≺ₒ Σ₁ ≺ₒ Σ₂ ≺ₒ … ≺ₒ Σ<sub>R</sub>, with Σ<sub>R</sub> ∈ O<sub>F</sub>

The important relation is not that the trajectory draws a closed shape. It is that the resultant configuration Σ<sub>R</sub> again satisfies the independently specified criterion F defining O<sub>F</sub>, the identity whose reference-bearing role is A<sub>F</sub>.

A melody can return to its tonic without the final acoustic pressure field being numerically identical to the opening one. The analogy illustrates only the logical distinction between returning to a structural role and reproducing every underlying state variable.
