# 20.7 The Electromagnetic Geometry We Carry Forward

From Chapter 19 we inherit electricity and magnetism as emergent expressions of relational degrees of freedom that are spatially independent while remaining dynamically coupled:

$$\text{spatially independent} + \text{relationally coupled} \rightarrow \text{orthogonal electromagnetic organization}$$

Spatial independence allows distinct directional modes to coexist. Relational coupling allows those modes to take part in one energetic structure. Their orthogonal organization is not a geometric decoration added to light after the fact; light inherits it from the electromagnetic structure through which it propagates.

Four results from Chapter 19 carry directly into luminous propagation:

1.  **Independence as zero overlap.** A change expressed entirely along one relational direction has zero projection onto the other. Once metric structure has been earned, this gives the geometric condition for orthogonality (20.17).

2.  **Transverse organization in the free-propagation regime.** In the regime defined in 20.3, neither part has a component along the direction of travel. In three spatial dimensions that leaves the plane across the travel, which holds exactly two independent orientations (20.17).

3.  **Signed addition.** In the free-propagation regime, fields add point by point with sign, so they pass through one another unchanged and negate one another where they meet out of sync (20.21).

4.  **Mutual driving.** Each part's change drives the other, with two coupling strengths that together set the propagation speed (20.19).
