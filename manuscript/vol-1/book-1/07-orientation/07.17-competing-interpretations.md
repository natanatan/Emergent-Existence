# 7.17 Competing Interpretations

Primitive direction. The simplest alternative treats directed relations as primitive. That makes orientation easy and weakens the project’s parsimony, since source and target asymmetry is inserted rather than derived. This chapter rejects the route unless simulation shows derived orientation is impossible under the allowed ontology.

Absolute spontaneous symmetry breaking. A stronger origin story begins from a literally homogeneous, perfectly symmetric Ω and invokes spontaneous symmetry breaking. Established physics shows symmetric laws can possess asymmetric states or solution branches, so the idea is not incoherent. Applying it to an absolutely structureless prephysical origin would exceed what this framework can justify, which is one reason the origin of 1.5 is defined by resolution rather than by absolute absence.

Hidden fine structure. The opposite extreme posits a detailed microstructure beneath Ω. That relocates the explanatory burden and risks smuggling in the very asymmetries the book means to derive. The chapter permits unresolved variation only as a possibility; it assumes no specific hidden entities, dimensions, directions or dynamics.

Purely epistemic orientation. A fourth reading treats orientation as an observer’s choice of ordering. That becomes inadequate once asymmetric dependency has interventionally different consequences for closure or return. Observer labels may describe orientation; the non-equivalence of admissible transformations supplies its ontic content.
