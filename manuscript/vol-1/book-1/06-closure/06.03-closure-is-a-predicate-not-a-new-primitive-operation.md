# 6.3 Closure Is a Predicate, Not a New Primitive Operation

The project’s parsimony rule forbids solving the closure problem by introducing an unexplained operator whose output is simply “closed.” The cleanest notation is therefore predicative rather than generative:

▪ Closed<sub>F</sub>(ℛ) = true or false

This notation says that ℛ satisfies a stated closure criterion relative to F. It does not add a process called Closed<sub>F</sub> to our ontology. In particular, it must not be confused with the mathematical notion of a closure operator. In standard mathematics, closure operators are typically extensive, monotone and idempotent. Closed elements are fixed points of the relevant operator. That formal family is useful as an analogy for completion. Closure needs no new operator C with C(ℛ)=ℛ.

The same discipline applies to 𝒰. Distinction typed 𝒰 fundamentally as an admissible transition relation on configurations. Later domains may admit branching, so Closure must not silently replace that relation by a globally single-valued update map:

▪ (Σn, Σn+1) ∈ 𝒰

Closure may be preserved or destroyed along admissible succession under 𝒰, but closure is not another meaning of 𝒰. Nor should 𝒰 be stretched into a Boolean, membership verdict, or aggregation operation. The transition relation constrains what may succeed what; the closure predicate describes a property some configurations and admissible trajectories possess.

▪ admissible succession under 𝒰 ≠ logical verdict ≠ closure predicate

This distinction becomes important again when boundary aggregation is revisited in 6.9.
