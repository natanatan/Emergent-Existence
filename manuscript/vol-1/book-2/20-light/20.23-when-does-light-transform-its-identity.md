# 20.23 When Does Light Transform Its Identity?

Some changes preserve continuity:

$$\text{continuous re-instantiation} \rightarrow \text{identity preserved}$$

A reflected excitation can remain continuous with its incident history. A refracted one can remain continuous while its path, wavelength and phase accumulation change. Polarization can rotate, frequency can shift, amplitude can decrease and the envelope can spread, without severing the history that defines the excitation.

Other changes reorganize the energy strongly enough that the previous closure is no longer continuously available:

$$\text{reorganization into a new admissible closure} \rightarrow \text{identity transformed}$$

Absorption is the clearest example. The energy does not disappear; it enters another organization.

A further possibility leads toward matter:

$$\text{open propagating closure} + \text{additional relational constraint} \rightarrow \text{possible persistent local closure}$$

Measurement constrains this possibility. Pair creation, in which light produces an electron and its oppositely charged partner, has been registered close to atomic nuclei (Anderson 1933; Blackett and Occhialini 1933†). The ATLAS experiments at CERN registered light scattering off light (2017) and the production of pairs of W particles from light (2020)†, in both cases from the fields of charged ions or protons passing close to one another without colliding. External charge is present in these measured cases, but we do not treat it as a necessary condition for light to interact with light. Our proposal locates the relevant charge structure in the light itself: if each luminous excitation carries opposed internal charge states whose sum is zero (20.17, ◌), two excitations can couple through that internal structure while each remains neutral overall.

The two accounts stand differently here. The standard account predicted light scattering off light in the 1930s (Halpern 1933; Heisenberg and Euler 1936)†, about eight decades before it was registered, so its reading is predicted. Our internal-charge reading was formed after these measurements and has no quantitative test yet, so it is untested.

The next ontological question is therefore no longer how light propagates. It is how energetic organization becomes locally self-retaining.
