# 12.28 A Projection-Stability Research Program

Once a candidate effective geometry emerges, probe it in multiple ways. Let F<sub>1</sub> through F<sub>n</sub> differ in coupling architecture, resolution threshold, sampling path, and coarse-graining strategy while remaining admissible under independently declared criteria.

For each frame calculate O<sub>i</sub> = Π<sub>Fi</sub>(I<sub>Fi</sub>(𝔏)). Extract the invariant family H from each effective result. The hypothesis predicts approximate agreement of H across the regime in which a common geometry is claimed.

▪ H(O<sub>1</sub>) ≈ H(O<sub>2</sub>) ≈ … ≈ H(O<sub>n</sub>)

A geometry that appears only under one specially tuned probe is weak evidence. A geometry whose rank, neighborhood relations, and curvature diagnostics survive diverse probing is substantially stronger.

This test operationalizes the chapter’s central epistemic stance: an observed representation can be frame-relative and lossy while the invariants recovered across frames remain objective constraints on the underlying organization.
