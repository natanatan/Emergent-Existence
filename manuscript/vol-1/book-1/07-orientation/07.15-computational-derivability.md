# 7.15 Computational Derivability

The computational burden is sharper than “can arrows appear?” A valid model must begin with admissible transition relations that encode no preferred orientational branch and test whether higher-order organization produces persistent non-equivalence under reversal. A minimal program proceeds in seven steps.

Initialize an ensemble of relational states symmetric at the chosen resolving level, allowing either unresolved microvariation or symmetry-related perturbations without directional labels.

Apply a symmetric, equivariant admissible transition relation 𝒰, and verify computationally that the relation itself does not privilege a branch under the declared exchange transformation g.

Detect emergent closed organizations using Closure’s criteria, with F specified independently of the target orientation.

Within each closure, test whether dependency interventions produce K<sub>ij</sub> ≁ K<sub>ji</sub>. External diagnostics may use counts or probabilities even though those quantities are not yet ontological primitives of the modeled system.

Test persistence: does the asymmetry survive enough recursive updates to alter the closure’s return condition?

Reverse the discovered orientation structure. Does the reversed organization still permit re-entry into O<sub>F</sub> under the same identity criterion F?

Compare against matched controls in which apparent orientation is created only by labels, initial directional bias or hard-coded asymmetric rules.

The strongest success condition would be a family of models in which symmetric rules generate symmetry-related candidate branches. However, recursive closure and return produce robust branch-specific persistence without a preferred direction being supplied by the programmer.

Falsification guardrail. If stable orientation appears only when the admissible transition structure, the initial resolved state, the organizational anchor A<sub>F</sub>, or a target topology already encodes the eventual direction, the derivation has failed. If every apparent asymmetry vanishes under role exchange once labels are removed, orientation has not been earned.
