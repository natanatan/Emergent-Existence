# 20.1 Why Light Matters

We ordinarily describe human perception through five familiar senses: sight, hearing, touch, taste and smell. Yet much of the world we practically differentiate is organized through vision. Distance, shape, motion, boundary, color, expression, written language, tools, hazards and much of our shared environment become available to us through what light lets us see.

Vision is only one way of registering light. Seeing belongs to living observers, and each species sees its own band: the human eye responds to wavelengths from roughly 380 to 750 nanometres (nm)†, bees respond further into the ultraviolet, and pit vipers sense infrared†. Instruments register far wider bands. A radio dish, an X-ray telescope and a photon counter in a laboratory each register light the eye never sees, and each becomes useful to us when it translates what it registers into an image, a sound or a number we can see. Almost everything known about the universe beyond Earth has arrived this way. Every chemical bond, every act of photosynthesis and every signal in a fibre-optic cable is also an exchange with the same field.

In the vocabulary of Book I, a difference that can be recovered somewhere other than where it arose is a recoverable distinction. Here, information means exactly that. Light is one of the principal ways distinctions made in one place become recoverable in another: information propagating across space.

A beam crossing a dark room appears simple. It seems to extend from one place to another. But whatever light is, it must preserve enough organization to remain physically identifiable while continually leaving one spatial neighborhood and entering another. Direction, phase, frequency, amplitude and polarization all remain recoverable on arrival, even though the excitation carrying them is never stationary.

We are therefore trying to understand how energetic relational structure can preserve an identity through propagation.

We begin from structures already earned. Space is an active relational geometry (Book I, Chapter 13; Chapter 18). From Chapter 19 we inherit electricity and magnetism as coupled relational degrees of freedom organized within that geometry. Light becomes possible when those resources combine into a form of energetic organization that closes recursively while remaining open to spatial hand-off.

$$\text{electromagnetic organization} \rightarrow \text{open recursive closure} \rightarrow \text{coherent propagation across space}$$

The central question is therefore physical before it is optical: what organization allows energy to remain itself by continually going somewhere?
