# The Origin Inheritance Ledger

Origin earns almost nothing, and that is its purpose: it fixes what every later chapter may not assume. This ledger records, for each chapter that follows, what Origin passes forward and what it withholds. It is offered as an audit aid. A reader who suspects that a later chapter has borrowed structure it did not earn can check that chapter’s row here, and then the chapter itself.

| **Later chapter** | **What Origin passes forward** |
| --- | --- |
| Difference | Ω as the minimally resolved initial reference condition; logical rather than temporal priority; the prohibition against reading Ω as a place, moment, substance, cause, observer or anchor. |
| Distinction | The requirement that admissible succession, if introduced, must not be read as physical time; Proposition 1B’s second commitment remains explicit rather than hidden in the language of distinction. |
| Boundary | Resolution-relative undifferentiation; failure to resolve does not establish ontological identity or absence; Proposition 1A supplies the burden that observable structure requires retained distinction. Ω is not a privileged pole. |
| Relation | The prohibition against importing objecthood or geometry into primitive relation; higher-order structure must arise from mutually constraining retained distinctions rather than pre-existing objects. |
| Closure | Originhood is a role rather than a unique first moment. A closed higher-order identity may earn a new origin/reference role through representative-independent addressability, yielding A<sub>F</sub> without inheriting Ω as an anchor. |
| Orientation | No spatial or primitive direction is supplied at Origin. Orientation must arise from consequential non-equivalence of relational roles after an organizational reference has been earned. |
| Return | Origin, anchor and return are distinct. Return concerns re-entry relative to a Closure-earned A<sub>F</sub> and identity class; recurrence remains pre-temporal. |
| Formalism | The metalanguage may be richer than the ontology; mathematical representation must not read unearned structure back into Ω or the early relational stages. |
| Scale | Origin supplies no magnitude, unit or ruler. Comparative magnitude must be earned from recursive organization and endogenous standards. |
| Distance | No metric separation is available at Origin. Distance must arise from earned relational mediation and scale rather than being read into the primitive dyad. |
| Geometry | No coordinate system, topology, dimensionality or embedding is available at Origin. Geometry is constructed only after distance relations acquire stable mutual constraint. |
| Space | No physical place or extension is available at Origin. Space requires geometrically coordinated localization to become physically consequential. |
| Time | No physical duration, causal cone or temporal order is available at Origin. Time may use retained dependency only after Boundary and must add the conditions needed to turn weak precedence into an order that warrants temporal interpretation. |
| Book II | Physical spacetime, dynamics, fields and matter remain beyond Book I. Book II must test whether the separately earned structures of Space and Time can become physically instantiated rather than merely formally coherent. |
