# 12.18 When Multidimensional Properties Become Stable

Now suppose many admissible probes differ in their raw representations but repeatedly require the same number of independent local parameters to preserve the geometric relations under study. One frame uses coordinates (x<sup>1,</sup> <sup>…,x</sup>n); another uses (x<sub>prime</sub><sup>1,…,x</sup><sub>prime</sub>n). The labels differ, yet reducing the representation below n independent modes destroys distinctions that both frames recover.

That provides a second and operational candidate for effective dimension, alongside the structural one of 12.6:

▪ d<sub>probe</sub> = minimum number of independent parameters required to preserve the declared geometric invariants

The two need not coincide. d<sub>R</sub> counts the independent modes the organization itself supports; d<sub>probe</sub> counts the parameters a family of probes cannot do without. They agree when every structural mode is separately resolvable, and the framework has not shown that they must. The definition is operational but not arbitrary. The invariants must be specified independently, and the dimension must remain stable under admissible changes of probe, representation, and scale over the regime in which the claim is made.

This also gives us a crucial ontological distinction:

▪ projected dimension ≠ fundamental dimensional complexity

An observed three-dimensional geometry would establish that three independent modes suffice for the stable geometric relations resolved in that regime. It would not, by itself, prove that the underlying organization contains only three relevant relational degrees of freedom at every scale.
