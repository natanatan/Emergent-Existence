# 9.22 Independence Before Orthogonality: Introducing dR

Relation raised the need to distinguish independent relational modes. Formalism is the first place a candidate symbol d<sub>R</sub> can be introduced without pretending that vector-space rank was already available. The required first step is therefore an independence structure, not a number.

Matroid theory is useful because it abstracts a notion of independence from linear algebra. Let V<sub>R</sub> be a set of candidate relational modes and 𝒥<sub>R</sub> a family of subsets satisfying whatever independence axioms the model can justify. Write the candidate independence system as Ind<sub>R</sub> = (V<sub>R</sub>, 𝒥<sub>R</sub>). This remains representational until the required axioms are shown to arise from the relational organization itself.

Only if a suitable finite independence structure is earned may the framework introduce a rank-like count as a candidate pre-metric relational dimensionality: d<sub>R</sub> := rank(Ind<sub>R</sub>). This is the first legitimate introduction of d<sub>R</sub> in the derivation. It counts independent relational modes under the declared independence criterion; it does not yet mean spatial dimension, vector-space dimension or orthogonal axes.

▪ independence can be formalized before orthogonality

◌ SPECULATIVE d<sub>R</sub> as a matroid-rank-like count of independently variable relational modes. Orthogonality would require additional structure, typically an inner product or an equivalent earned construction, and is not licensed merely because later physics may use it.
