# 5.5 Constraint Propagation and Recursive Incorporation

A retained distinction becomes more structurally consequential when its consequences propagate through the domain. Because 𝒰 is fundamentally an admissible transition relation rather than a generally available function, write two successive admissible configurations as:

▪ (Σn, Σn+1) ∈ 𝒰, (Σn+1, Σn+2) ∈ 𝒰

Suppose Σn carries a retained contribution associated with ra and removing that contribution changes the admissible possibilities available to rb in a later configuration. Then ra has not merely persisted as a stored mark. Its retained consequence participates in the dependency structure of the domain. Boundary already supplies the schematic relation Σn ≺ Σn+2 when such removal changes what remains admissible.

▪ retained r<sub>a</sub> ⇒ Δ𝒫(r<sub>b</sub> \| ℛ)

This permits a stronger interpretation of retention than pairwise preservation alone requires. Persistence need not mean that an earlier realization remains unchanged. A relation can disappear in its original carrier while its retained consequence remains encoded in later admissibility. Repeated succession can be represented by relational composition 𝒰^k in the analyst’s formalism, but that notation does not turn 𝒰 into a primitive function or introduce physical duration. What matters here is whether some organization remains identifiable across the admitted succession and declared tests. That question leads directly to invariance.

○ INTERPRETIVE Constraint propagation allows a retained distinction to persist through consequences rather than through literal preservation of its original carrier.
