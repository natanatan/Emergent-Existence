# 7.21 Implications and Handoff

We entered an exchange-symmetric relational world and leave with a derived bearing. Persistent consequential asymmetry can justify oriented dependency without making direction primitive.

That changes what we can follow. A relational transformation can now have an ordered route through its own dependency structure.

Return follows such a route past its starting identity and asks a new question: can the organization become that identity again without undoing everything that happened along the way?

▪ A<sub>F</sub> ≺ₒ Σ₁ ≺ₒ Σ₂ ≺ₒ … ≺ₒ Σ<sub>k</sub>, with Σ<sub>k</sub> ∈ O<sub>F</sub>

The next question is not whether the sequence comes back later in metric time. It is whether an oriented sequence can re-satisfy the identity criterion of its anchor, what distinctions survive that re-entry. Whether return itself generates a new higher-order relational invariant.

Handoff to Return. Under what conditions can a sequence of relationally ordered dependencies re-enter the identity class of its anchor without erasing the orientational consequences accumulated through the sequence?

Questions Opened by This Framework

What is the weakest formally non-circular definition of K<sub>ij</sub> that captures constraint propagation without presupposing causality, probability, metric or time?

Can the origin condition Res<sub>W₀</sub>(Ω) = ∅ be stated without making Ω observer-dependent or covertly positing inaccessible microstructure?

What mathematical structure should represent resolving depth before physical scale is derived: nested equivalence relations, partitions, sigma-algebras, category-theoretic quotients, or something simpler?

Can a symmetric, equivariant admissible transition relation generate higher-order closures with K<sub>ij</sub> ≁ K<sub>ji</sub> when no preferred orientation is encoded at the resolved initial level?

What kinds of unresolved variation suffice for asymmetry to cross a higher-order resolution threshold, and which merely average out?

Is return compatibility a general selection mechanism or only one route by which asymmetry becomes persistent?

Can anchor-relative irreversibility be defined entirely through equivalence classes and admissible transformations, without binary success variables such as Ret(A<sub>F</sub>; 𝔒) = 1?

Are derivation order, retained dependency order and derived orientation the same relation at different levels, or irreducibly distinct?

What conditions let local orientations compose into a globally consistent structure, and what arises when they cannot?

How should d<sub>O</sub> be defined before linear algebra and orthogonality are earned, and how does it relate to d<sub>R</sub>?

Can directed cycles preserve orientation while supporting a return invariant that later becomes phase or winding?

What further constraints are necessary before oriented dependency becomes a partial order, proto-causality or temporal order?

Could apparent large-scale symmetry itself be emergent from many lower-order asymmetries, and how would the framework distinguish symmetry restoration from unresolved asymmetry?

What would falsify the claim that orientation is derived rather than primitive in the proposed computational program?
