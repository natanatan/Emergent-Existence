# 11.18 A Familiar Case

Take the five loci of Figure 11.1, with their everyday counterparts: A is Lagos, B is Doha, C is Perth, D is Nairobi and E is Colombo. The admissible mediating relations are A–B, B–C, A–D, D–E and E–C, each a single flight and each belonging to the same magnitude class u.

The first chain from A to C contains two equivalent mediators; the second contains three.

▪ L<sub>u</sub>(A–B–C) = 2u

▪ L<sub>u</sub>(A–D–E–C) = 3u

The candidate distance is therefore:

▪ d<sub>u</sub>(A,C) = 2u

Lagos to Perth by way of Doha is the least mediated route, and that is what the construction has selected.

The figure also carries the distances flown, and they run the other way. The two-mediator chain covers 5 900 km and then 9 000 km, for 14 900 km; the three-mediator chain covers 3 800, 5 200 and 5 100, for 14 100 km. The route with fewer mediators is the longer journey by 800 km.

▪ fewest mediators ≠ least ground covered

Both statements are true, and neither is a correction of the other. They answer different questions, and each is a magnitude only relative to the standard that defines it. Under u, where every admitted flight counts alike, d<sub>u</sub>(A,C) = 2u. Under a standard in which each flight carries a weight proportional to the ground it covers, the weighted construction of 11.7 selects the other chain, and the distance from Lagos to Perth becomes 14 100 km by way of Nairobi and Colombo.

▪ change the magnitude standard, and the minimum may change route

Nothing in the example tells us that the loci occupy Euclidean space. No coordinates have been assigned, and no line has been drawn through a physical background: the kilometres are borrowed from the everyday reading, not derived by the framework. What the framework has is narrower. Given a declared standard, the least internally admitted relational mediation between A and C has a definite magnitude, and changing the standard is a change of question rather than a discovery that one answer was wrong.
