# 5.8 Underlying State, Identity Equivalence and Frame-Resolved State

The preceding example exposes another ambiguity. Two configurations can be non-identical while satisfying the same identity criterion, and a resolving frame can in turn fail to discriminate configurations that differ in identity-relevant ways. Three relations must stay separate:

▪ underlying non-identity ≠ identity equivalence ~<sub>F</sub> ≠ frame equivalence ~<sub>W</sub>

Let W be a resolving frame in Boundary’s sense. Two underlying configurations may be distinct, Xa ≠ Xb, while W fails to resolve them apart, Xa ~<sub>W</sub> Xb. Separately, an identity criterion F may classify two configurations as realizations of the same higher-order identity, Xa ~<sub>F</sub> Xb, even when W resolves many differences between them. Neither equivalence entails the other. This is Boundary’s explicit separation of discriminatory access from identity carried into structured domains.

A thermal example is useful only as an illustration of effective description. A temperature reading or thermal sensation can integrate several lower-order physical relations into one effective state without implying that those lower-order states are identical. At the present ontological level the narrower lesson is simply that a resolving criterion may coarse-grain distinctions while an identity criterion tracks a different set of preserved features.

▪ Xa ~<sub>W</sub> Xb does not imply Xa ~<sub>F</sub> Xb

Both equivalences are indexed and criterion-relative. There is no license to infer identity simpliciter from either one, and no license to make observation constitutive of object-like identity.

○ INTERPRETIVE Sameness at an emergent level may consist in preservation of identity-relevant organization under F even while a resolving frame W continues to discriminate lower-order differences.
