# 20.18 Energy in Units at Interaction

Measured: the energy delivered per absorption is hf, the same slope h at every frequency (20.3). The question for this account is why the energy delivered should rise in a straight line with the recurrence rate of the closure.

We can separate the proposed answer into two parts. First is the question of where the unit lives. Units of energy are resolved only at interaction (◌). Between emission and absorption, light is field excitation traversing space (20.8), and no fixed packet of energy travels from source to absorber. The unit belongs to the interaction that closes the excitation: the absorber can take energy only in its own fixed steps, so energy is delivered in amounts of hf. Lamb and Scully (1969) showed that the photoelectric results follow from exactly this arrangement.

The second part is a candidate mechanism for the slope (◌). For circularly polarized light, the measured rotational push and the energy delivered stand in the ratio L = E/ω, where ω = 2πf (Beth 1936; Poynting 1909†). If each closing interaction transfers one fixed unit of handedness, ħ, then E = ħω = hf follows at every frequency, and Planck's constant becomes 2π times that unit. The burden then moves to one geometric question: why the handedness transferred per closing interaction is fixed.

That question must be kept separate from $L_{\text{closure}}$, the angular momentum of the recursive return. In 20.9 the return tightens as energy rises. Whatever $L_{\text{closure}}$ does as the return tightens, the rotational push that registers per absorbed unit is ±ħ at every frequency (20.3). So if $L_{\text{closure}}$ grows with energy, it cannot be what registers as rotational push at absorption; if it does register, it cannot grow. Which of these holds, and what measurable consequence $L_{\text{closure}}$ has, is open (Questions 3 and 5).

By the same bar: the standard account meets E = hf by postulate (Planck 1900, Einstein 1905). Neither account yet derives the value of h.
