# 3.5 Symmetry Covariance: No Hidden Labels

Let Aut(𝔇) denote the automorphisms of the primitive dyad. At the two-pole minimum, the nontrivial automorphism exchanges a and b. Primitive admissibility must respect that symmetry:

▪ (x, y) ∈ 𝒰 ⟺ (σx, σy) ∈ 𝒰 for every σ ∈ Aut(𝔇)

This is the precise form of the “no analyst-imposed labels” rule. An admissibility fact cannot depend on which pole the theorist happens to call a. If the structure licenses a nontrivial transition from one pole to the other, symmetry covariance licenses its exchanged counterpart as well.

Nontriviality requires at least one transition between non-identical configurations:

▪ ∃x, y ∈ 𝒞<sub>𝔇</sub> : x ≠ y and (x, y) ∈ 𝒰

For the symmetric dyad, one such transition plus covariance forces reciprocity.
