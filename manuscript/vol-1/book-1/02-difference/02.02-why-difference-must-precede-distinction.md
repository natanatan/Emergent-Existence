# 2.2 Why Difference Must Precede Distinction

To distinguish is already to do more than state that two terms are non-equivalent. Distinction implies that the non-equivalence can enter an operative contrast, a test, an update, or some other internally consequential structure. If the framework begins with Distinction as the primitive, it risks hiding that extra burden inside the verb “distinguish.”

This order refuses that compression. Difference names the austere structural fact of non-equivalence. Distinction, reserved for Chapter 3, will require an additional commitment that makes the non-equivalence usable in succession.

▪ non-equivalence ⇝ admissible succession ⇝ retention ⇝ consequence

The squiggle arrow ⇝ is derivational only (A.2).
