# 9.18 Binary Relations May Not Be Expressively Sufficient

The early ontology is written with pairwise relations r<sub>ij</sub>. However, not every higher-order constraint is necessarily reducible to pairwise relations without adding auxiliary entities. General relation theory permits n-ary relations, R(q₁, q₂, …, q<sub>n</sub>). A graph privileges binary incidence; hypergraphs and general relational structures let one relation involve several relata jointly.

Two routes stay open. Under binary sufficiency, every higher-order constraint can be represented without loss by pairwise relations, possibly with auxiliary relational positions. Under genuine higher arity, some constraints are more faithfully represented as irreducibly n-ary at the level being modeled.

▪ binary convenience ≠ binary ontological sufficiency

The question should not be settled by convenience, and it is worth saying plainly what settling it would cost. Relation’s matrix formulation and Orientation’s constraint relation K<sub>ij</sub> are both pairwise. If higher arity turns out to be required, those are the sections that change. This makes this one of the few open questions in Book I with a known repair bill.
