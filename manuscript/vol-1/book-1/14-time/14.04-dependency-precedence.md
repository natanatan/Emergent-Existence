# 14.4 Dependency Precedence

We write the dependency relation as:

▪ Σₐ ≺ Σᵦ

where ≺ means generative or retained-dependency precedence. The intended reading is not yet “Σₐ occurred at an earlier physical time than Σᵦ.” It means that Σᵦ contains consequences whose form depends on structure retained from Σₐ, such that removing the relevant retained contribution changes Σᵦ or the admissible path to it.

A useful counterfactual criterion is therefore:

Σₐ ≺ Σᵦ only if removing the retained contribution inherited from Σₐ changes Σᵦ or changes the admissibility of a dependency chain leading to it.

This prevents mere notation from generating order. Writing states in a list does not make them temporally ordered. The order must be recoverable from dependency structure itself.

Boundary does not grant asymmetry as a global property of ≺. A particular dependency may be one-way, but reciprocal or cyclic retained dependence is not excluded at this stage. Therefore:

▪ Σₐ ≺ Σᵦ does not by itself settle whether Σᵦ ≺ Σₐ

Where one-way retained dependence is recovered, it supplies a candidate ingredient of temporal direction. Where mutual reachability occurs, Time must not force a before-and-after relation onto the cycle. The distinction between one-way precedence and mutually dependent closure becomes the burden of the next section.
