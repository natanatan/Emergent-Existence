# 14.3 Inheritance from Retained Distinction

Boundary established that a distinction can remain available after transformation and that retaining or removing it can change the admissible possibility set 𝒫. It then defined retained-dependency precedence ≺ between configurations without assuming that relation to be transitive, acyclic, total, causal, or temporal. Time inherits exactly that weaker structure.

▪ 𝒫(Σ, r) ≠ 𝒫(Σ, ∅)

The formal point is modest. A relational configuration can carry inherited structure whose removal changes what remains admissible. Repeated occurrences of such dependence generate a directed dependency graph, but a directed graph is not automatically a temporal order: it may contain branches, merges, incomparable events, or cycles.

This is the bridge Time needs. No clock has appeared. No temporal coordinate has appeared. What has appeared is counterfactually consequential retained dependence. The remaining burden is to determine when the dependency organization itself supports a stable order rather than merely a network of mutual constraints.
