# 7.12 Local Orientation, Global Orientability and Cycles

Local orientation does not imply a single global orientation. A domain may contain several non-equivalent directed dependencies without admitting one consistent global ordering. This matters because a directed cycle is oriented even though it cannot be represented as a global acyclic order:

▪ A ≺ₒ B ≺ₒ C ≺ₒ A

The cycle does not erase orientation; it shows that orientation is weaker than partial order. In graph theory, orienting an edge simply assigns a direction. At the same time, stronger global properties such as strong connectivity or acyclicity impose further constraints. Robbins’ theorem characterizes when an undirected graph admits a strongly connected orientation. The relevance here is formal rather than ontological (Robbins, 1939).

The important cycle for this framework is anchor-relative. Orientation earns the relation ≺ₒ; Return will ask what it means for an oriented sequence to depart from and later re-enter O<sub>F</sub>, whether re-entry is exact or merely identity-equivalent, and what path-dependent structure survives it.
