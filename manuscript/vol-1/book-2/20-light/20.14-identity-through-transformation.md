# 20.14 Identity Through Transformation

Which changes can a propagating excitation undergo while remaining the same excitation, and which break its continuity?

$$\text{same light excitation} \Leftrightarrow \text{the open closure is continuously re-instantiated}$$

Frequency can shift. Amplitude can change. Polarization can rotate. Direction can bend. The envelope can broaden, narrow or be partly redistributed. None of these necessarily ends the excitation's identity, provided the identity-bearing organization stays continuously recoverable.

This distinguishes transformation from replacement. An excitation can change substantially while remaining one continuous propagating history. If an interaction breaks that continuity and a new closure later forms, the new closure is a new excitation, even if some of its properties resemble the old one.

In every measured emission, scattering and absorption, the energy given up equals the energy received (20.3), even where an excitation's identity ends.

We can now carry forward a propagating excitation maintained by re-instantiation, a spatial medium in which every neighborhood is continually updated, and a direction stabilized by neighboring reinforcement. Several mechanisms remain proposals (◌): the possibility that local update and reconciliation complete through more than one cycle before hand-off, the tightening of the recursive return as the mechanism of frequency, the speed limit as a property of the medium, and the claim that units of energy are resolved only at interaction. These proposals must remain consistent with what has been measured.
