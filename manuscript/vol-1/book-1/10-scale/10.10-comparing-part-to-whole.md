# 10.10 Comparing Part to Whole

Suppose a higher-order organization W composed of two non-overlapping sub-organizations:

▪ W = A ⊔ B

The symbol is disjoint composition and implies no spatial separation. Write Cmp(X, Y) for the comparative magnitude relation of X to Y. This here means only what 10.4 and 10.5 earned. Pairing succeeds, or it fails one way, or it fails the other.

What we would like to say is that the whole stands to A as A stands to B:

▪ Cmp(W, A) ≈ Cmp(A, B)

The difficulty looks severe. Correspondence gives same, larger and smaller between two collections. Sameness of pattern across two different pairs looks like a further thing, and the obvious way to state it is with ratios, which we do not have. The apparent choice is to assert the pattern now and justify it later, or to wait.

Neither is necessary. Take one more building that correspondence already supports. Given two organizations X and Y, form the collection of ordered pairs drawn one from each, written X × Y. This needs nothing new: pairs were available from Distinction, and ordered pairs from Orientation. This earned the asymmetry of roles that makes a first and a second differ. Nothing is counted; a collection of pairs is exhibited.

Sameness of comparative form can now be stated as a single correspondence. The whole stands to A as A stands to B exactly when the pairs drawn from W and B match, one to one, the pairs drawn from A and itself.

▪ Cmp(W, A) ≐ Cmp(A, B) ⟺ W × B ↔ A × A

This is cross-multiplication, written as the bijection it always was. Read the familiar form to check it: W/A = A/B holds exactly when W·B = A·A. The product of two finite collections is the collection of their ordered pairs. No numeral, no ratio and no unit has been used. We have used pairing, ordered pairs, and pairing again.

The building states the part-to-whole case without a number anywhere in it. With W = A ⊔ B, the condition reads (A ⊔ B) × B ↔ A × A. The pairs drawn from the whole and the smaller part match the pairs drawn from the larger part with itself.

▪ Proportion is definable by correspondence alone; ratio, when it arrives, represents a relation that pairing had already fixed. Open question: whether the construction extends beyond finite organizations, where products and bijections are unproblematic.
