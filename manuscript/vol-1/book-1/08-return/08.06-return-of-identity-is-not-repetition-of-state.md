# 8.6 Return of Identity Is Not Repetition of State

The strongest distinction here is between identity-class recurrence and exact state repetition.

▪ Σ<sub>R</sub> ∈ O<sub>F</sub> does not require Σ<sub>R</sub> = Σ<sub>A</sub>

Σ<sub>R</sub> may preserve the organization constitutive of O<sub>F</sub> while differing in relations that are not part of F. This makes possible change internal to preserved identity:

▪ Σ<sub>R</sub> ∈ O<sub>F</sub> and Δ(Σ<sub>A</sub>, Σ<sub>R</sub>) ≠ 0

The metaphysics of persistence has long distinguished numerical identity from qualitative similarity and has examined the criteria by which identity through change is assessed. The present framework borrows only the structural lesson: preservation of an identity criterion need not require preservation of every qualitative feature. The broader literature treats criteria of identity and of persistence as distinct philosophical problems (Noonan and Curtis, 2018).

For this chapter, Σ<sub>A</sub> and Σ<sub>R</sub> are distinct configuration tokens that may belong to the same relational identity class O<sub>F</sub>. That distinction becomes decisive if a later dependency order is required to remain acyclic.
