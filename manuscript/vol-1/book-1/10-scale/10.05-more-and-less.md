# 10.5 More and Less

The more informative case is when pairing fails. Suppose every member of A still pairs uniquely into B, but B has members left over. We need not know how many remain: the failure is itself the information, and it is an injection.

▪ f : A ↪ B

For finite organizations this says A is no larger than B. If A injects into B while B does not inject into A, B has the greater cardinal magnitude. The standard benchmark is the Cantor–Schröder–Bernstein theorem. If each injects into the other, a bijection between them exists.

▪ A ↪ B ∧ B ↪ A ⇒ A ~# B

We have an order of magnitude without a ruler.

▪ same, no larger, larger — all available before numerical labelling

We have moved from structures that differ to structures that differ in amount. This is a real transition rather than a notational one.
