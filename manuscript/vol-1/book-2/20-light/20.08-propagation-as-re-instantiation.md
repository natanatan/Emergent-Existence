# 20.8 Propagation as Re-instantiation

Once electromagnetic organization forms an open closure, propagation is the repeated re-instantiation of that geometry. In one neighborhood the update cycle completes the state that can be communicated forward. The neighboring region receives that organized constraint through its own electromagnetic relations. A compatible successor state forms, and the process repeats:

$$S_{i} \rightarrow  S_{i}^{*} \rightarrow  S_{i + 1} \rightarrow  S_{i + 1}^{*} \rightarrow \cdots$$

Here $S_{i}$ is the state of the propagating excitation in neighborhood $i$, $S_{i}^{*}$ is that state after its local cycles have completed it, and $S_{i + 1}$ is the newly instantiated state in the next neighborhood. The arrow from $S_{i}$ to $S_{i}^{*}$ is the local cycling (one or more cycles, 20.5); the arrow from $S_{i}^{*}$ to $S_{i + 1}$ is the hand-off.

What propagates is continuity of organized state. Energy, phase, orientation and electromagnetic structure are expressed through new local participants at every step, while the excitation itself remains identifiable by the criterion of 20.14. Light in transit is field excitation traversing space, and nothing more is needed to describe it. In particular, no packet carrying a fixed unit of energy travels from source to absorber: the unit is resolved only when an interaction closes the excitation (20.18).

This is the same recursive principle by which higher-order identity has survived lower-order replacement throughout the ontology. Light gives that principle a specifically physical and spatial form.
