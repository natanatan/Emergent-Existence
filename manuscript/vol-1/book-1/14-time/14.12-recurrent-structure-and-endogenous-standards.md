# 14.12 Recurrent Structure and Endogenous Standards

Metric time requires a repeatable standard. In ordinary physics, clocks supply such standards by exploiting approximately recurrent processes. A relational derivation should recover the logic of a clock without assuming a temporal parameter in the clock’s definition.

Suppose a subsystem K returns through a sequence of distinguishable relational configurations:

▪ K₀ ↦ K₁ ↦ … ↦ K<sub>m</sub> ≈ K₀

where ≈ means sufficiently equivalent at the resolution relevant to the clock function. If repeated cycles preserve their internal dependency structure under a range of external conditions, then another process can be compared against the number of recovered cycles.

▪ τ<sub>K</sub>(A,B) = n<sub>K</sub>(A,B) · u<sub>K</sub>

Here n<sub>K</sub> is the number of stable K-cycles occurring across the dependency interval between A and B, and u<sub>K</sub> is the conventional unit assigned to one cycle. The formula is not yet a claim that τ<sub>K</sub> equals proper time. It shows how a metric-like temporal quantity could arise from relational recurrence rather than from an independently flowing temporal substance.
