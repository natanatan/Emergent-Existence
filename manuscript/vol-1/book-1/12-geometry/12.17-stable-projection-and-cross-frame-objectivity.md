# 12.17 Stable Projection and Cross-Frame Objectivity

One probe result proves little about the substrate. If frame F<sub>1</sub> yields 𝔏<sub>1</sub> and frame F<sub>2</sub> yields 𝔏<sub>2</sub>, their disagreement can arise from different resolution, different coupling, different scale, or genuine structural variation.

But suppose independently constituted probes repeatedly recover the same higher-order invariants H even when their raw outputs differ. Then:

▪ H(𝔏<sub>1</sub>) ≈ H(𝔏<sub>2</sub>) ≈ … ≈ H(G<sub>n</sub>)

The common structure has stronger standing than any one representation. It is a candidate cross-frame invariant.

We may therefore define effective geometric objectivity as invariance across a sufficiently broad family of admissible probes.

▪ effective objectivity = cross-frame stability of declared invariants

This criterion is intentionally demanding. A family of identical instruments may share identical blind spots. Robustness should be tested across heterogeneous interactions whenever possible. Stable agreement is evidence for deeper organization; it is not proof that the resolved representation exhausts that organization.
