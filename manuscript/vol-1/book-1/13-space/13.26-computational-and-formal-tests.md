# 13.26 Computational and Formal Tests

The project’s methodological distinction between constitutive and diagnostic use is especially important here. Mature geometric tools may test the output of a generative model, but spatial coordinates, locality, dimensionality, or smooth manifolds may not quietly enter its lower-order update rules.

Coordinate-removal test. Strip coordinate labels, embeddings, drawing positions, and target spatial metadata. Attempt to recover the same localization relations intrinsically. If Space disappears when the coordinates disappear, Space was encoded.

Locality-prediction test. Generate geometry without specifying spatial adjacency as a target. Test afterward whether recovered geometric neighborhood predicts independently specified coupling accessibility better than randomized controls.

Perturbation-coordination test. Change one recovered localization and measure whether the surrounding localization relations change systematically rather than arbitrarily.

Probe-heterogeneity test. Apply substantially different resolution maps and determine whether candidate spatial invariants survive.

Scale test. Coarse-grain repeatedly and measure whether localization, adjacency, and dimensional rank remain stable over a finite regime.

Smoothness-failure test. Search for scales or regions in which a smooth approximation ceases to fit while higher-order localization remains recoverable.

Responsive-geometry test. Permit lower-order relational transformations to perturb the substrate without explicitly changing geometric variables. Ask whether the recovered effective geometry changes as a consequence.

▪ continuum failure ≠ localization failure
