# 5.19 What Would Count Against the Chapter

Mutual constraint cannot be defined without presupposing intrinsic objects whose properties do all the explanatory work.

The distinction between a collection of relations and an integrated structure collapses under formalization, so that the proposed criterion adds nothing beyond connectivity.

Structural persistence cannot be specified without a hidden temporal metric or a pre-existing physical substrate.

Relational re-instantiation reduces to an analogy that supplies no generalizable invariant criterion.

Object-like identity requires choices of F and diagnostic variation so free that any grouping can be made persistent by stipulation.

Higher-order discernibility cannot distinguish two internally equivalent structures without importing coordinates or primitive labels.

Multi-relation boundary aggregation requires an uncontrolled new primitive that cannot be reduced to, or constrained by, previously earned operations.

Relational degrees of freedom cannot be defined before metric or vector-space structure without becoming merely verbal.

Computational models show no robust difference between mutually constraining persistent clusters and generic network artifacts under perturbation.

The strongest single failure condition: if integrated structure cannot be specified without importing intrinsic objects, geometry, metric, orientation or an uncontrolled proliferation of new primitives, Relation has not completed its derivation.
