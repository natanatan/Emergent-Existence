# 20.18 Decoherence Is Not an Outcome

Three effects are easily run together, and the chapter's mechanism addresses only one of them.

First, interference can become unavailable because the field becomes correlated with its surroundings, as with the slit detector. This is *decoherence*, and it is well understood: once the difference between alternatives is recorded in the environment, the cross term cannot be recovered by looking at the system alone. Second, as a result, the statistics of the system become indistinguishable from an ordinary mixture of definite outcomes. Third, an individual detector acquires and keeps one definite record.

Decoherence explains the first two. It does not explain the third: after decoherence, standard quantum mechanics still describes the detector as a superposition of records, merely one whose parts can no longer interfere. The proposal of 20.9 addresses the third effect, and that is where its content lies. It may also speed up the second, if the feedback rapidly spreads the difference between alternatives, but that relation has to be modeled. An account that reproduced only the loss of interference would leave the measurement problem where it was found.
