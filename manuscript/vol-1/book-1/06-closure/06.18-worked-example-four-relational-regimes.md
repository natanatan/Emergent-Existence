# 6.18 Worked Example: Four Relational Regimes

A minimal example separates connectivity, circularity, structural closure and robust dynamic closure without introducing geometry.

| **Regime** | **Relational condition** | **Interpretation** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Connectivity | r<sub>ab</sub> and r<sub>bc</sub> share a relatum, but each remains independently admissible | A network exists; no integrated closure |
| B. Circular dependence | r<sub>ab</sub>, r<sub>bc</sub>, r<sub>ca</sub> form a cycle, but r<sub>ca</sub> also requires external x constitutively | The graph cycles; dependency closure fails |
| C. Structural closure | All F-maintaining dependencies are internal and jointly realizable | Closed at the current relational state |
| D. Robust dynamic closure | Regime C persists or recovers F under 𝒰 and across a preregistered nontrivial subset of 𝔗C | Strong candidate for a higher-order persistent identity |

Now suppose Regime D contains two lower-order configurations Σ<sub>a</sub> and Σ<sub>b</sub> satisfying Σ<sub>a</sub> ~<sub>F</sub> Σ<sub>b</sub>. A larger-domain relation E acts on each. If

▪ E(Σ<sub>a</sub>) ~<sub>F′</sub> E(Σ<sub>b</sub>)

then the larger domain can treat both as one higher-order O<sub>F</sub> for purposes of E. If the results fall into different higher-order classes, the proposed macro-object is not addressable under that coupling. The grouping must be refined or abandoned.

This example supplies an explicit failure mode. Higher-order unity is earned relation by relation. It is not guaranteed merely because an analyst can draw a boundary around a cluster.
