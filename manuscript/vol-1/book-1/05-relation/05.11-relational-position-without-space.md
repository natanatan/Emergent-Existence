# 5.11 Relational Position Without Space

The previous section yields a concept that resembles position without being spatial. OA and OB can satisfy the same internal identity criterion while occupying different relational conditions with respect to a larger resolving frame. Define:

▪ p<sub>A</sub> = λ<sub>W</sub>(O<sub>A</sub>), p<sub>B</sub> = λ<sub>W</sub>(O<sub>B</sub>), p<sub>A</sub> ≠ p<sub>B</sub>

The symbols p<sub>A</sub> and p<sub>B</sub> are not coordinates. They are relational positions: distinctive profiles by which the larger frame resolves each object relative to the rest of the domain.

Relational position is the distinctive relational profile an organization occupies within a larger resolving domain.

No anchor is required for this definition. A relational position may be fixed by a distributed profile across the larger domain rather than by relation to one privileged member. If two organizations have the same relation to one candidate reference but differ elsewhere in the domain, the richer profile can still distinguish them. Position is therefore a pattern of relations sufficient for frame-relative discernibility, not an intrinsic tag.

Relational position is not yet spatial: it has no line, interval or metric until Orientation and Distance.

Spatial position may eventually emerge from persistent non-equivalent relational positions, but Relation earns only the latter.
