# 11.2 Difference Is Not Separation

The framework has possessed non-identity since Distinction:

▪ A ≠ B

But two things can differ without their difference having magnitude. Two configurations may be non-equivalent; two higher-order identities may respond differently under the same transformation. None of this tells us how far apart they are.

▪ A ≠ B ⇏ d(A,B)

Distance requires more than distinction. It requires relational position.

Relation introduced position in a deliberately non-spatial sense: what an identity is relative to the organization around it. Here we extend that idea. Two identities are relationally separated when they occupy non-equivalent positions within an organization and the relation between those positions cannot be collapsed into identity under the declared criterion.

Call their pre-metric separation ρ(A,B). At this stage ρ is not a number. It says only that the organization distinguishes the positions of A and B relationally.

▪ relational separation ≠ metric distance
