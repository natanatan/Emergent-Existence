# A Note on the Computational Companion

The open entries in the Hypothesis Status Register are not all matters of argument. Several are claims about what a model would do, and those can be run rather than debated: whether recursive relational rules settle into a stable low-dimensional regime, whether diffusion and wave behaviour diverge on a relational graph, whether an effective metric survives coarse-graining, whether temporal order survives the removal of every index.

Those tests belong to a separate document. Specifying one properly takes more room than a table cell: the generative rule, the initial conditions, the control it is measured against, the declared diagnostic and its threshold, the number of runs, and the criterion that would count as failure. Compressed into a book appendix, each would become a sentence that sounds like a plan and could not be executed.

The companion is therefore published alongside this volume, addressed to readers in computer science and applied physics who intend to run the models rather than read about them. It carries the guardrails this book’s methodology imposes on computational work: that a model demonstrates a mechanism rather than a world, that the declared diagnostic and its control are fixed before the run, and that a failed diagnostic is reported rather than retuned. Where the companion produces a result, the Register entry it bears on is updated here.

What matters for the present volume is the commitment rather than the code. A framework that claims to be constrained by more than its own consistency should say what would embarrass it, and say it precisely enough that someone else can check.
