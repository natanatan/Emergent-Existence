# 6.7 Structural Closure and Dynamic Closure

The distinction between structural and dynamic closure separates a snapshot property from a trajectory property. A configuration can be structurally closed at one derivational state and fail immediately under the next update. That is too weak to ground persistent higher-order identity.

Let ℭF denote the class of relational configurations satisfying the structural closure conditions for F. Admissible succession under 𝒰 then permits a stronger question along a realized or selected trajectory:

▪ Σn ∈ ℭF and (Σn, Σn+1) ∈ 𝒰 ⇒? Σn+1 ∈ ℭF

When the implication holds across the relevant admissible succession, closure is dynamically preserved along that trajectory. Dynamic closure therefore does not redefine 𝒰. It is a property of a sequence of configurations licensed by 𝒰. A stronger requirement over every admissible successor would be a robustness or invariant-set condition and should be stated separately.

This is where the persistence requirement belongs. A structurally closed state that fails at the next update has satisfied the three conditions of 6.4 and no more. Persistence is preserving those three under 𝒰.

Working distinction. Structural closure is internal sufficiency of an identity-relevant organization at a relational state. Dynamic closure is the preservation or recurrent recovery of that sufficiency under the system’s endogenous state-to-state updates.

▪ structural closure + preservation under 𝒰 ⇝ dynamic closure

This brings the chapter close to dynamical-systems language without yet importing its full apparatus. Fixed points, invariant sets, limit cycles, basins and stability spectra belong in Formalism. Here the important idea is more primitive. A closure that performs ontological work must survive some transformation rather than exist only as a static coincidence.

▪ Closure can be tested against 𝒰 without making closure another operation of 𝒰. Open question: whether dynamically persistent closures arise generically under admissible update rules or only under rules designed to produce them.
