# 5.7 Carrier Persistence Is Not Structural Persistence

Once structural persistence is separated from state persistence, a further distinction follows. A relational pattern can remain identifiable even when its physical or representational carrier changes. Engineered information systems demonstrate the logical form without establishing anything about fundamental physics.

Consider an encoded work stored in one hardware state, decoded into a media encoding, transmitted. Reconstructed as an audiovisual pattern available to a human. The successive realizations are not physically identical; their microscopic states can differ radically. Yet selected relations are preserved well enough that the later realization is recognized as an instance of the same organized content.

▪ H ↦ D ↦ M ↦ P, with H ≠ D ≠ M ≠ P and H ~<sub>F</sub> D ~<sub>F</sub> M ~<sub>F</sub> P

Here H is a hardware realization, D an encoded organization, M a decoded media structure, P a perceptually accessible realization. ~<sub>F</sub> denotes equivalence with respect to the selected relational invariant F, not physical identity. This motivates a term used later in the series.

Relational re-instantiation is the production of a new state or realization whose organization preserves selected relational invariants of an earlier structured state.

The example establishes only logical coherence. Human-made encodings are designed to preserve specified relations; they do not show that nature works the same way. Their narrower value is to show that persistence of organization does not require persistence of carrier. A theory of object-like identity need not equate sameness with material identity. The philosophy of mind reached the same conclusion under the name multiple realizability (Putnam, 1967; Fodor, 1974). A functional organization can be realized by physically different substrates. This section borrows the form of that argument without its psychological content.
