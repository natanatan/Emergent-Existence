# 13.13 Dimension Becomes Spatial Dimension

Geometry has already earned independent collective modes of variation, represented schematically as δ₁, δ₂, …, δₙ. Nothing in Geometry required that every such mode correspond to physical spatial extension.

A geometric mode becomes a spatial dimension when variation along that mode changes localization within the coordinated physical domain.

▪ geometric independent mode + localization consequence ⇝ spatial dimension

▪ d<sub>relational</sub> ≠ d<sub>geometric</sub> ≠ d<sub>spatial</sub>

A richer substrate can therefore possess more organization than any bounded frame resolves as physical spatial dimensionality.

Cao, Carroll, and Michalakis (2017) provide a useful neighboring comparison: they construct an emergent geometry from entanglement relations and use multidimensional scaling to recover best-fit spatial dimensionality. Their proposal begins with Hilbert-space structure, so here it serves diagnostically: it shows a route for testing whether non-spatial organization can yield an effective spatial manifold.
