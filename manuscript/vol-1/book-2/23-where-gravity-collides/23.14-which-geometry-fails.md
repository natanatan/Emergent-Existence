# 23.14 Which Geometry Fails

This chapter placed the end of the quantum regime at the point where alternatives would condition the field into incompatible local geometries. A physicist will ask at once which geometry is meant.

If it meant the geometry of general relativity, the gravitational curvature of spacetime, the claim would face an immediate difficulty. In the double slit experiment, the gravitational effect of a single photon or electron is unmeasurably small, and spacetime around the apparatus is flat to many decimal places. Yet the dot still forms. Gravitational curvature alone cannot be what localizes the outcome in an ordinary detection, as Penrose's own estimates for small masses show.

The chapter's answer is that the geometry in question is the relational one: the neighborhoods, mediation chains and effective dimensions earned in Chapters 11 to 13, of which large-scale gravitational geometry is one coarse expression (Chapter 22). The detector is not a single particle. Its excitation is a new retained organization in a large, many-part system, and the alternatives at issue are alternative conditionings of that system's relational neighborhood. The threshold is therefore a property of the coupled field and detector together, not of the photon alone. This is how the proposal can keep Penrose's logic while applying it where his gravitational estimate would give no effect.

The cost of that answer is a precise criterion. A smooth effective description of a region is closed so long as the distinctions it suppresses make no difference to what happens next. It fails when two states that the description treats as the same lead to measurably different continuations once finer structure is resolved. The proposal needs a measure of how incompatible two local conditionings are, and a value of that measure at which retention happens. A changing effective dimension, unstable neighborhoods, or conflicting local constraints could each signal such a failure; none of them is yet that measure. Until it is defined, "incompatible local geometries" names the right kind of quantity without fixing it.

A related distinction must be kept. When several local conditionings overlap, the descriptive unit may have to become one joint state of the field rather than separate pieces acting on a shared background. Overlap alone does not force this; many conditionings sit together in one geometry without trouble. And even where a joint description is needed, joint description is not yet entanglement. What distinguishes entanglement is the Bell behavior of 23.20.
