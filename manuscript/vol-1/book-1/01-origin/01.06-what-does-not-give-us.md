# 1.6 What Ω Does Not Give Us

A thin starting condition is useful only if we resist enriching it by intuition.

Ω is not a place. A place requires distinguishable positions, and distinguishable positions have not been earned.

Ω is not a moment. Earlier and later require an ordering that has not been given a temporal interpretation.

Ω is not a substance. Nothing is said to be made of Ω, and no internal parts have been specified.

Ω is not a cause. It exerts no force, initiates no mechanism, and supplies no dynamics merely by being named.

Ω is not an observer. “Reference” does not mean a viewpoint possessed by a mind.

Ω is not an anchor. Difference and Distinction preserve exchange symmetry and Boundary constructs identity without a center. A reference role is earned only later, when Closure produces a dynamically persistent higher-order identity whose representative-independent addressability can function as the organizational anchor A<sub>F</sub>.

Ω is not a spatial point. A mathematical point can be defined without coordinates in some formalisms, but ordinary language strongly suggests location. This framework therefore avoids treating Ω as a point until a later geometry has earned that interpretation.

Ω ≠ (x, y, z, t)

These negatives do not tell us what ultimate reality is. They tell us what we are not yet entitled to use.

That is the discipline of the opening chapters: subtraction first, construction second.
