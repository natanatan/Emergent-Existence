# 5.6 Relational Invariance and Structural Persistence

Suppose an identity-relevant criterion F of the relational organization remains satisfied across a declared organization-level diagnostic family 𝔗R, a specialization of Boundary’s diagnostic transformation family rather than an additional internal update law. Then:

▪ F(T(Σ)) = F(Σ) for every T ∈ 𝔗R

We do not yet specify what F must be. It might concern connectivity, a pattern of participation, an algebraic constraint, or a more sophisticated invariant introduced later. The point is methodological: structural persistence requires stating both what is identity-relevant and under which declared variation it is tested. The diagnostic family does not tell the system how to update; it asks whether already-earned organization survives variation. This immediately separates two notions often conflated:

▪ state persistence ≠ structural persistence

A state persists when some particular configuration remains. A structure persists when identity-relevant organization survives even though constituents or local values change. A whirlpool is not offered as proof of our ontology, only as the logical form. The pattern persists while the material occupying it changes. Section 5.7 makes the same point without a fluid. An invariant with no specified transformation class is rhetorically impressive and formally empty. Weyl’s treatment of symmetry (1952), which makes invariance under a stated group the content of objectivity, is the nearest classical statement of the same discipline.

▪ Structural persistence is preservation of identity-relevant relational organization through an explicitly stated diagnostic transformation family. Open question: what the weakest sufficient criterion is, and which later physical dynamics, if any, select a privileged realized transformation regime rather than leaving 𝔗R purely diagnostic.
