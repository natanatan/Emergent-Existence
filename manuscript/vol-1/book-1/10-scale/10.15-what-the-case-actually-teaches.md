# 10.15 What the Case Actually Teaches

The integers are not the interesting part. The interesting part appears when we compare each term with the one before it. Writing r<sub>n</sub> = F<sub>n+1</sub>/F<sub>n</sub>, the recurrence gives:

▪ r<sub>n+1</sub> = 1 + 1/r<sub>n</sub>

If the ratios approach a positive fixed value r, then r = 1 + 1/r. As a result, r² = r + 1, whose positive solution is:

▪ φ = (1 + √5)/2

The lesson is exact and modest. The magnitudes keep changing while the relation between successive magnitudes settles.

▪ changing magnitude ⇝ stable inter-level proportion

That is why φ belongs here at all. It is an established example of recursive change producing stable relative magnitude. This is precisely what scale was defined to be.
