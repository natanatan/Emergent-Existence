# 14.13 Clockhood as a Relational Role

A subsystem is not a clock because time flows through it. It functions as a clock when its recurrent transformations provide a sufficiently stable comparison standard for other transformations. Clockhood is therefore a role that a relational subsystem can acquire.

A candidate clock K should satisfy at least four conditions:

recurrence: the system repeatedly returns to a comparison-equivalent state;

countability: cycles or phases can be distinguished and retained;

stability: the relevant cycle structure remains sufficiently invariant across the domain of use;

couplability: other processes can be compared with the clock without the comparison relation itself silently defining the desired temporal result.

The last condition is easy to violate. If the model defines every process by reference to the clock variable from the outset, then agreement with the clock has been built in. A genuine relational time standard must be cross-checkable against independently organized recurrent processes.
