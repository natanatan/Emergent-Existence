# 12.29 Competing Interpretations

The same successful effective geometry can support several interpretations. One may regard the geometry itself as fundamental. Another may regard it as emergent from non-geometric relations. A third may propose several metric-relational layers whose coupling produces the effective structure. A fourth may treat geometry primarily as a compact language for stable invariants without committing to geometry as a separate ontological entity.

This chapter does not decide among those possibilities merely by introducing multilayer notation. Its candidate claim is conditional: if independently constrained relational layers generate stable low-dimensional metric organization whose invariants survive scale change and heterogeneous probing, then effective geometry can be explained without assuming its familiar dimensional form at the foundation.

That would thin the ontology. If the construction fails and stable geometry requires an irreducible geometric primitive, the framework should stop the reduction there rather than conceal the failure behind new terminology.
