# 6.6 Dependency Closure and Consistent Realizability

A simple cycle is not enough. Let three relations be represented as r<sub>ab</sub>, r<sub>bc</sub> and r<sub>ca</sub>. A graph can contain the cycle

▪ r<sub>ab</sub> ≺ r<sub>bc</sub> ≺ r<sub>ca</sub> ≺ r<sub>ab</sub>

Here ≺ is the retained-dependency relation inherited from Boundary, applied to constitutive dependencies among retained relations; the cycle is not orientational and therefore does not use ≺ₒ. While one of the relations still depends constitutively on an unspecified external condition x:

▪ r<sub>ca</sub> = f(r<sub>ab</sub>, r<sub>bc</sub>, x), x ∉ ℛ

The representational graph closes, but the identity-relevant dependency does not. Conversely, three mutually specified equations can be syntactically circular yet inconsistent. If no relational configuration satisfies them simultaneously, no realized organization exists to be closed.

Let 𝒮<sub>F</sub>(ℛ) denote the set of configurations of ℛ that satisfy the constraints constitutive of F. A necessary realizability condition is simply:

▪ 𝒮<sub>F</sub>(ℛ) ≠ ∅

This notation carries no metric, probability measure or state-space geometry. It means only that the stated constraints are jointly satisfiable. For a configuration that actually obtains, the condition is met automatically. It has force when a proposed specification of F, or a model of it, is being tested, since a specification that no configuration can satisfy describes no organization at all.

Robert Rosen’s work on closure to efficient causation provides a useful higher-level analogue (Rosen, 1991). In the biological setting, closure concerns whether the productive causes required by the organization are themselves entailed within the organization rather than supplied indefinitely from outside. Later formal work has sought computable realizations of this idea (Cárdenas et al., 2010). The present chapter does not import Rosen’s causal vocabulary into the prephysical ontology. It borrows only the structural question: where do the dependencies required by the organization come from?
