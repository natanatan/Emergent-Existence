# 14.9 Chains, Branches, and Histories

A temporal chain is a set of mutually comparable quotient events under ≺ₜ. Along such a chain, the derived order supplies an unambiguous succession:

▪ \[e₀\] ≺ₜ \[e₁\] ≺ₜ \[e₂\] ≺ₜ … ≺ₜ \[eₙ\]

A branch occurs when one event constrains more than one admissible successor. A merge occurs when one event depends on retained consequences from more than one predecessor. These structures matter because physical histories need not be simple strings.

▪ branch: \[eₐ\] ≺ₜ \[eᵦ\] and \[eₐ\] ≺ₜ \[e𝚌\], with \[eᵦ\] and \[e𝚌\] incomparable

▪ merge: \[eₐ\] ≺ₜ \[e𝚌\] and \[eᵦ\] ≺ₜ \[e𝚌\], with \[eₐ\] and \[eᵦ\] possibly incomparable

The candidate temporal structure therefore resembles a directed dependency network more naturally than a universal timeline. A familiar one-dimensional time coordinate, if it later becomes useful, would be a higher-order representation of suitable portions of this order rather than the primitive source of the order.
